## Regulatory / Market Context
- **CPPA's first Delete Act enforcement actions** (August 11–13, 2026): California fined LocateSmarter, LLC $116,490 for failing to register as a data broker and requiring Social Security numbers for opt-outs. Cybba, Inc. was fined $52,400 for the same registration failure. These are the first enforcement actions under both the CCPA and Delete Act — a significant precedent signaling active enforcement posture from California.
- **California data broker registration fee increase**: CPPA voted to raise registration fees from $6,000 to $9,500, increasing financial barriers for smaller data brokers.
- **DROP platform processing obligations**: Effective August 1, 2026 — data brokers must now process DELETE requests every 45 days. This mandated activity directly supports the value proposition of opt-out services like Incogni and Privacy Bee.
- **Connecticut's data broker law** (signed May 27, 2026): Requires data broker registration, creates new consumer deletion rights, and restricts surveillance pricing. Now 20 US states have comprehensive privacy laws — the geographic coverage of regulatory pressure is expanding.

## Market Summary
The most significant development of the week is **Surfshark Group's acquisition of Optery (September 7)**, which consolidates the two most automation-forward data removal brands (Incogni + Optery) under one parent — alongside a **security incident disclosure** affecting the same parent, which will test consumer trust. The regulatory calendar is accelerating: California's first Delete Act enforcement fines are now on record, the DROP platform is live and processing, and Connecticut's new law signals a state-by-state regulatory expansion that structurally favors managed removal services over DIY approaches.
