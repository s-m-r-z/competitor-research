# Competitor Insights — 2026-08-10

## Incogni
- **New "Protect" plan** bundles automated data removal (420+ brokers) with NordProtect identity theft protection — includes credit monitoring, dark web monitoring, identity remediation, and insurance coverage. US-only (excludes NY and WA for insurance component).
- NordProtect Platinum subscribers now get Incogni included directly in the plan, further blurring the line between VPN, security, and data removal into a unified Nord Security bundle.
- Pricing: Standard plan at $7.99/month (annual) or $15.98/month; Unlimited at $14.99/month; Protect plan pricing not widely published yet.
- Strategy signal: Incogni is moving upstream toward identity protection — expanding beyond removal-only into a full security product suite.

## Kanary
- **Kanary Copilot** (launched October 2025) continues to gain review attention as a differentiating mobile-first offering — available free on iOS/Android with premium at $9.99/month.
- Covers 150+ brokers; hybrid model where Kanary handles most opt-outs automatically but users complete CAPTCHAs for some resistant brokers.
- Positioning shifts the brand from traditional desktop SaaS toward an "online safety copilot" concept targeting mobile-native consumers.
- 300+ broker coverage cited in broader Kanary brand comparisons vs. the Copilot app-specific 150+ figure — suggests tiered product architecture.

## DeleteMe

- Consumer Reports continues to cite DeleteMe as a "mid-level performer" (≥65% removal rate), trailing Optery and EasyOptOuts in head-to-head testing.
- Catalogue covers ~600 US people-search aggregators with 90-day re-check cycles.
- No new product announcements or pricing changes detected in the last 48 hours.

## Optery
- Four-tier model: Free (self-serve scans), Core ($39/yr, 100+ sites), Extended ($149/yr, 200+ sites), Ultimate ($249/yr, 350–600+ sites).
- Remains the top performer in Consumer Reports testing alongside EasyOptOuts; known for before-and-after screenshot transparency proving removals.
- Canadian market expansion (January 2026, excluding Quebec) remains a competitive moat — no other major US service has followed.
- No major new announcements in the last 48 hours.

- Covers 1,128 data brokers; differentiates via legal escalation (formal complaints, regulatory filings) for non-compliant brokers — a tactic no direct competitor currently matches at scale.

## Regulatory: California DROP Enforcement — Technical Details Emerge
- DROP enforcement (live as of August 1, 2026) uses a **standardized hashing protocol**: data brokers must format fields identically before hashing (dates as 8-digit strings, phone numbers as last 10 digits without dashes, etc.) before comparing against submitted consumer records.
- This eliminates the long-standing broker excuse of "the data submitted doesn't match our records" — a loophole that had historically undermined DIY opt-outs.
- Over 600 registered brokers must access DROP every 45 days and process the now 300,000+ queued deletion requests. Fines: $200/day per unprocessed request.
- Impact on industry: The friction DROP removes from the opt-out process could reduce perceived consumer need for paid removal services among California residents — services will need to differentiate on speed, non-CA broker coverage, and ongoing monitoring.

## Market Summary
The dominant story today is Incogni's push into identity theft protection with its NordProtect-bundled "Protect" plan — a significant strategic escalation that repositions the service as a full security product rather than a point solution. Meanwhile, California's DROP system continues to mature technically (standardized hashing closes broker loopholes), raising the regulatory floor for all brokers and putting pressure on the value proposition of subscription removal services for California residents.
