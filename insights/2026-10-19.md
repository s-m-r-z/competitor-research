# Competitor Insights — 2026-10-19

## DeleteMe
- Pricing as of mid-2026: $129/year (1 person), $229/year (2 people), $329/year (4 people); no monthly option available.
- Standard plan covers ~100 US-focused brokers; higher tiers claim 750+ brokers.
- Claims 100 million+ personal listings removed since 2010 (vendor-stated figure).
- Re-submits removal requests quarterly; acts as an authorized agent under CCPA.
- Some brokers require optional government ID upload to complete removal — a potential friction point vs. competitors.
- California's Delete Act (DRPA) — launched August 2026 — lets CA residents opt out of 543 registered brokers in one step; analysts suggest this may reduce paid-service value for CA customers.

## Privacy Bee
- **Acquisition (August 2026):** Privacy Bee acquired EraseMe.app to expand data removal technology and ongoing privacy monitoring. Existing Privacy Bee subscribers are unaffected during integration; EraseMe.app customers are being migrated.
- **Perfect PCMag score (August 5, 2026):** Privacy Bee became the first and only data removal service to earn a 5/5 Editors' Choice rating from PCMag. Competitors Optery and Incogni scored 4.5.
- Claims coverage of 1,128 data brokers — the broadest stated coverage in the market.
- ZDNET named it one of the most comprehensive removal services in a DeleteMe comparison.
- No October-specific announcements found; August acquisition and rating are the headline items.

## Kanary
- Published a 2026 Product Brief positioning itself as combining proprietary tech with human analysts for continuous monitoring, removal, and prevention.
- Tiered pricing: free Community plan, $9.99/month Professional, $50/month Advanced (managed/fully automated).
- Coverage: ~300–350 data brokers depending on source; also covers people-search sites, court records, and social exposure.
- Community and Professional tiers still require user action via the mobile app for some removals; Advanced is the fully hands-off option.
- Geography: Community/Professional are US-only; Advanced extends to the UK.
- Reviewer takeaway: strong for monitoring and transparency; weaker on full automation vs. Incogni.

## Incogni (Surfshark)
- **New pricing tier added September 5, 2026:** CostBench changelog notes a new plan was added, expanding the range to $7.99–$22.99/month. Specific tier details not yet confirmed from primary sources.
- Current known plans: Standard at $7.99/month ($95.88/year); Individual Unlimited at ~$179.88/year (includes custom broker requests); Family plans around $149–$191/year for up to 5 people.
- 420+ brokers covered; standard broker list independently audited by Deloitte.
- 30-day money-back guarantee; no free trial. Serves US, UK, EU, Switzerland, and Canada.
- TechBullion comparison concludes Incogni better for automated prevention; Kanary better for monitoring visibility.

- Completed SOC 2 Type II attestation — a trust/compliance differentiator vs. less credentialed peers.
- Ranked No. 12 on Inc. Regionals: Pacific list with 763% revenue growth over two years (company-touted; April 2026).
- Covers 270–640 data broker sites depending on source/plan tier; free scan available.
- Reviews praise screenshot evidence of removals; value depends on upgrading past the free tier.

## Market & Regulatory Summary
- **Data broker opt-out obstruction:** Congressional investigation (February 2026) found several brokers (Comscore, Telesign, 6sense, IQVIA, Findem) had hidden opt-out pages from Google search — four made changes after pressure; this boosts the case for managed removal services.
- **California Delete Act (August 2026):** Data brokers now required to delete data within 90 days of a request via the state's DRPA platform. Advocates note that under 1% of Californians have exercised these rights, suggesting consumer awareness remains low — an opportunity for paid services to market ease-of-use outside CA.
- **JEC Senate Report (February 2026):** Estimated data broker breaches cost consumers ~$21 billion; called for stronger federal oversight — regulatory tailwind for the removal service category.

## Market Summary
The data broker removal space saw meaningful consolidation and credentialing activity in Q3 2026: Privacy Bee's EraseMe.app acquisition and perfect PCMag rating put it ahead on coverage claims and third-party validation, while Incogni quietly added a new pricing tier in September. OneRep continues to suffer reputational fallout from the Mozilla split, and California's Delete Act broadens free alternatives for US consumers — a pressure point that could compress growth for US-only services like DeleteMe.
