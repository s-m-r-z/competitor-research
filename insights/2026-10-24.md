## Regulatory & Market Environment
- **Connecticut:** Public Act 26-64 took effect October 1, 2026 — data brokers must register with the Commissioner of Consumer Protection beginning January 1, 2027; significant new compliance burden for brokers operating in CT.
- **California DROP Platform:** As of August 1, 2026, California's Delete Act requires data brokers to process deletion requests through the state's DROP platform — a structural tailwind for opt-out services.
- **Vermont & New Jersey:** Both states signed new data broker registration laws in June 2026 (effective Jan 1, 2027), adding to multi-state compliance pressure on brokers.
- **Federal:** 49 privacy bills are active in Congress as of October 2026; no comprehensive federal broker law enacted yet; disputes over state preemption continue.
- **Pricing landscape:** Industry-standard annual plans range $95–$180/year; market remains subscription-driven due to broker data re-collection cycles.

## Market Summary
The regulatory environment is accelerating in favor of data removal services, with Connecticut's new data broker law taking effect October 1 and California's DROP platform now live — both increasing opt-out complexity for consumers and demand for managed services. Optery's lingering OpenAI data-sharing controversy continues to be a differentiator opportunity for competitors emphasizing strict data minimization.
