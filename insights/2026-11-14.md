# Competitor Insights — 2026-11-14

## DeleteMe
- Marketing claims coverage of 750+ data brokers, though independent reviews estimate it handles roughly 100 US-focused brokers more rigorously.
- Pricing is reported at $129/year (Individual) and $269/year (Family); no price changes announced in the last 48 hours.
- Differentiates on human reviewers who handle complex opt-out cases requiring manual forms, fax, or notarized documentation.
- Has positioned DROP (California's centralized opt-out platform) as complementary to its service, arguing DROP works better *with* DeleteMe coverage still active.
- Claims 100M+ removals in self-reported marketing; no independent verification found.

## Incogni
- Owned by Nord Security (merged with Surfshark in 2022); covers 420+ data broker sites per official listing.
- No pricing or feature changes detected in the last 24–48 hours.
- Maintains strong positioning as the VPN-bundled privacy service; popular comparison target for mid-range buyers.

## Privacy Bee
- Self-reports coverage of 400–1,000+ data brokers; independent audits place the real figure closer to 200–400.
- No new announcements or pricing changes found in the last 48 hours.
- Continues to compete on breadth-of-coverage marketing claims vs. Incogni's more transparent broker list.

## Kanary
- Covers approximately 300 data broker sites, including major people-search aggregators.
- Recently published a blog post analyzing what happens when a data removal service shuts down — a likely competitive move to highlight the Removaly closure.
- No pricing or feature changes detected in the last 48 hours.

## Removaly
- Status: effectively defunct. Kanary's public content references the service going offline. Prior to shutdown it was acquired by DuckDuckGo in 2023.
- No new activity.

## OneRep
- **Notable (May 2026):** Expanded coverage to 550+ non-public data brokers; available through the OneRep API and included in Pro plans.
- Ongoing reputational risk from a 2024 Krebs on Security investigative report linking its CEO to people-search sites it claims to remove users from.
- No new announcements in the last 48 hours; last major product news was the May 2026 broker-coverage expansion.

## Optery
- **Notable (ongoing controversy):** Optery added OpenAI-powered AI processing for Removals Reports, defaulting users into data transfers to OpenAI. After backlash from privacy-focused communities (including removal from Privacy Guides' recommended list), Optery reversed the default and made the data transfer opt-in only.
- Now offers an internal processing option that does not use third-party AI systems.
- Optery states no customer data is used to train AI models and all screenshots are deleted from OpenAI after report generation.
- Despite controversy, CNET reportedly named Optery the best overall data removal service of 2026 (vendor claim — independent verification pending).
- Pricing: $39/year (Core) to $249/year (Ultimate) per third-party comparison as of October 2026.

## Emerging Services
- **CyberForget:** A new entrant claiming 200+ sites covered, $19.99/month or $99/year (Personal), $199/year (Family). Markets automatic re-removal as a differentiator. Only source found is vendor-authored content on dev.to — no independent reviews confirmed.

## Regulatory / Market Context
- **California DROP milestone (August 1, 2026):** Data brokers became legally required to process opt-out requests submitted through California's DROP platform as of August 1, 2026. Roughly 300,000 Californians have signed up. Only ~30% of registered data brokers were compliant in the platform's first enforcement week. Non-compliant brokers face fines of $200/request/day.
- **JEC Senate Report (February 2026):** Found major data brokers were deliberately hiding opt-out pages from search engines. Following a WIRED report and congressional pressure, brokers removed "noindex" tags — but the broader data-collection ecosystem remains intact.
- **Market size:** Data broker industry estimated at $294B in 2025, projected to grow to $450B by 2031, underscoring continued demand for opt-out services.
- **Re-listing problem:** No service provides permanent removal; data brokers continuously re-acquire and re-publish records, keeping subscription-based removal models relevant.

## Market Summary
The data broker opt-out space remains highly active on the regulatory front, with California's DROP enforcement deadline (August 2026) reshaping competitive positioning — services like DeleteMe are framing DROP as complementary rather than a substitute. The most significant competitor-specific development is Optery's OpenAI data-sharing backlash, which temporarily damaged its reputation but was mitigated by making AI processing opt-in and offering an on-premises alternative; this episode may create a short-term trust gap that rivals can exploit in messaging.
