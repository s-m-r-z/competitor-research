# Competitor Insights — 2026-10-26

## DeleteMe
- International enrollment appears closed as of late September 2026; the introductory international plan page no longer lists any countries, signaling a retreat from non-US markets.
- A 2024 Consumer Reports test gave DeleteMe a 27% removal rate — the weakest of major services tested. Optery and EasyOptOuts scored 68% and 65% respectively. These figures are aging but remain widely cited in current reviews.
- Standard plan automatically scans ~100 sites; 566 additional registries require custom manual requests — a workflow disadvantage vs. more automated competitors.
- Current pricing: $129/yr (1 person), $229/yr (2 people), $329/yr (household of 4).
- Multiple current reviews note that California's free DROP platform (enforceable since August 1, 2026) is now a credible no-cost alternative, which DeleteMe's own blog acknowledges.

## Incogni
- **Cybersecurity Awareness Week promotion (October 24, 2026):** Incogni launched a 58% discount campaign via 9to5Mac and other tech media, with a promo code — an aggressive seasonal push timed to awareness week.
- **Surfshark acquires majority stake in Optery (September 2026):** Incogni's parent Surfshark expanded its privacy portfolio by buying into a direct competitor. This may signal consolidation moves across the sector.
- Coverage at 420+ brokers on standard plan; Unlimited tier adds 2,000+ custom sites.
- An All About Cookies reviewer reported 89% completion rate (850/959 requests) as of October 2026.
- A 2025 peer-reviewed PETS Symposium study ranked Incogni highest among four tested services (76.6% removal rate), though all services were called "relatively ineffective" as a group.
- Deloitte limited-assurance report on Incogni processes (covering period through July 2025) provides third-party credibility.

## Optery
- Acquired by Surfshark (majority stake) in September 2026 — significant ownership change that could accelerate feature investment and international expansion.
- TechRadar named it a top service in 2026; offers a free tier, making it a low-barrier entry point.
- Core plan priced at $3.99/month or $39/year per one comparison source — among the lowest in the market.
- No third-party verification of removal methodology published as of recent reviews.
- Consumer Reports 2024: 68% removal rate (Optery Ultimate tier).

## Privacy Bee
- **PCMag perfect 5-star rating (August 6, 2026):** First data removal service to receive a perfect score from PCMag, earning Editors' Choice — a significant marketing milestone ahead of Incogni and Optery (both rated 4.5 stars).
- **Acquired EraseMe.app (August 12, 2026):** EraseMe.app customers being migrated to Privacy Bee's platform; EraseMe's technology integrated into the service. This expands Privacy Bee's technical capabilities and customer base.
- Automated removals and advanced features (search result support, home image blurring, monitoring) gated behind paid plans.

## OneRep
- Coverage claims vary widely by source: 876 sites (317 people-search + 559 non-public brokers) per one source vs. ~200 automated opt-outs per another. Discrepancy suggests marketing inflation of broker counts.
- Differentiates by rescanning to verify removals before marking them complete.

## Kanary
- Coverage described as 300+ data brokers; some sources note the count includes search engines and social media platforms, not just traditional brokers.
- Consumer Reports 2024: 34% removal rate — among the lowest tested.
- 2025 PETS study: 23.4% average removal — lowest of four services tested.
- Pricing discrepancy between sources ($9.99/mo vs. $250/yr) suggests active pricing tests or tiering.

## Market Summary
The week of October 20–26, 2026 was dominated by Incogni's Cybersecurity Awareness Week promotional push (58% discount), Privacy Bee's consolidation of its PCMag 5-star win and EraseMe.app acquisition, and the broader shadow of Surfshark's September purchase of Optery — pointing to accelerating consolidation in the space and increasingly aggressive seasonal marketing. California's DROP platform, now fully enforceable since August 1, continues to raise the bar for what free alternatives offer, pressuring all paid services to differentiate on automation quality and coverage breadth.
