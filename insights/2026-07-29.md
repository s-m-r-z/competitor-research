# Competitor Insights — 2026-07-29

## DeleteMe
- Remains a recognized name but ranked as a "mid-level performer" in 2026 independent testing, successfully removing data from 122 sites with a manual, risk-prioritized approach
- Current pricing starts at $6.97/month (annual) for individual plans — competitive but undercut on features by newer rivals
- Cumulative milestone: 50 million data removals over 10 years; trails Incogni's 200M+ pace significantly
- No major product announcements or pricing changes detected in the last 48 hours

## Privacy Bee
- Recent media placements: Travel + Leisure (July 20, 2026) and Daily Mail (July 1, 2026) keeping brand visible heading into summer
- Shifted from a single flat annual price to a three-tier model — Essentials, Pro, and Signature — ranging from $8.00–$67.00/month, now offering monthly billing, multi-year discounts, and multi-person coverage
- Claims coverage of 1,000+ data brokers plus 182,674 custom sites; adds Google search result removal, house blurring, a tracker-blocking browser extension, and Gmail inbox scanning as differentiators
- Positioning: strongest "set-and-forget" premium option; dark web monitoring and map blurring on top tier

## Kanary
- Pricing at $14.99–$16.99/month (billed annually); materially cheaper than prior years
- Coverage: 300+ data brokers plus Google/Facebook/social site sweeps via patented search; also offers detailed transparency reports and proof-of-deletion dashboards
- Performance concern noted in recent testing: only 6 profiles removed in the first two weeks — slow start compared to faster automated rivals
- No new feature launches or pricing changes detected in the last 48 hours

## OneRep (Onerep)
- **Major expansion (May 2026):** Added coverage of 550+ non-public data brokers, addressing behind-the-scenes personal data circulation beyond publicly visible people-search profiles — the largest single coverage expansion seen in the market this year
- Scans 318 publicly visible data broker sites with monthly re-monitoring and auto re-submission if data reappears
- Ongoing reputational risk: founder holds an ownership stake in Nuwber, one of the data brokers the service claims to protect users from; flagged in multiple 2026 reviews
- No new announcements in last 48 hours

## Incogni
- **New Protect plan:** Bundles Incogni with Coveron (formerly NordProtect), expanding the value proposition beyond pure data removal
- No longer requires power of attorney from users — a friction reduction that likely improves sign-up conversion
- Newly launched Unlimited tier adds custom removal requests for any website, not just registered data brokers
- Independent Deloitte audit continues to verify removal practices — strong trust signal vs. competitors
- Pricing: $7.99–$45.98/month depending on tier; family coverage for up to five members
- Industry stat: 200 million+ successful removals reported — dwarfs DeleteMe (50M/10 yrs), Onerep (19M), and Optery (30M)
- Named best personal data removal service by Expert Consumers for 2025/2026; consistent PCMag and PCWorld recognition

## Optery
- **Award wins (April 2026):** Named a winner in three 2026 Cybersecurity Excellence Award categories — Attack Surface Management, Anti-Phishing, and Human Risk Management — boosting B2B credibility
- Top performer in independent 2026 testing alongside EasyOptOuts; both exceeded 65% successful removal rate, outpacing DeleteMe, IDX, and Kanary
- Core plan at $39/year is the strongest entry-level value in the market; Ultimate plan covers 635+ automated sites plus 950+ total with custom removal requests
- Uses patented search technology and provides before-and-after screenshots as proof of removal
- No new announcements detected in the last 48 hours

## Removaly
- Effectively defunct: multiple sources indicate the service went inactive as early as 2022 and has not demonstrated active removal processing
- Advisories from 2026 publications warn users to verify operational status before subscribing
- No meaningful competitive presence in the current market

## Emerging Services & Market Trends
- **EasyOptOuts:** Gaining traction as a top performer alongside Optery in 2026 testing; annual subscription model with quarterly removal rounds
- **PrivacyOn:** Emerging enough to draw direct head-to-head comparison with Removaly; growing profile in independent review sites
- **CrabClear:** Appearing in roundup lists as a newer entrant
- **California DROP deadline (August 1, 2026):** Mandates that all ~566 registered California data brokers check the DROP deletion platform every 45 days and honor requests within 90 days — penalty of $200/request/day for non-compliance. Over 215,000 consumers already registered. This is the most significant regulatory event of the year for the industry; services marketing to California residents should expect demand spikes
- **Federal regulation:** SECURE Data Act and Online Privacy Act of 2026 both in early committee stages — no comprehensive federal privacy law yet
- **Market size signal:** An estimated 26 million Americans are projected to start using a data broker removal service in 2026, reflecting sharp year-over-year growth

## Market Summary
Incogni continues to extend its lead through aggressive coverage expansion, a new bundled Protect plan, and independent audit credibility, while Optery's strong testing performance and affordable pricing make it the top challenger — but the biggest near-term market force is California's DROP compliance deadline on August 1, 2026, which is poised to drive a surge in consumer demand for removal services across the board.
