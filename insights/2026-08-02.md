# Competitor Insights — 2026-08-02

## California DROP — Day 2 of Enforcement

- Enforcement crossed from mandatory on August 1; data brokers must now access DROP every 45 days and process deletion requests within 90 days
- Over 215,000 consumer registrants are already in the queue waiting on broker action; any broker that fails to comply faces **$200/consumer/day** in uncapped fines
- No competitor has issued a press release, blog post, or social campaign capitalizing on DROP's first compliance day — the marketing opportunity window remains open for a first mover
- For paid removal services, DROP's gaps remain the core value proposition: no coverage of non-CA brokers, international exposure, people-search sites outside the CA registry, AI-derived inferences, or ongoing monitoring with reporting

## Optery

- No new announcements in the last 24–48 hours
- Key recent feature: **AI-powered removals via OpenAI** — differentiates Optery as the first major player to publicly attribute an AI integration for the removal pipeline itself (not just for scanning)
- Maintains broadest claimed scan coverage: 1,120+ sites scanned, 635+ with automated removal, 1,120+ with custom requests
- Consumer Reports #1 ranking and PCMag Editors' Choice (4 consecutive years) remain active marketing assets; Summer 2026 20%-off promotion (code SUMMER2026) still appears active
- No pricing changes detected

## Incogni

- No new announcements today
- Continues to hold the strongest trust credential in the market: independent **Deloitte assurance audit** of removal practices — still no other major competitor has secured third-party verification of this kind
- 420+ broker coverage; renewal requests sent every 60 days (public) / 90 days (private); most affordable fully-automated service at $7.99/month (annual, 55% discount active)
- Family Unlimited plan at $275.88/year; 30-day money-back guarantee
- Positioned by multiple independent rankings (Fossbytes, Cybernews, AllAboutCookies) as the best value option in 2026

## DeleteMe

- No new announcements or pricing changes in the last 24–48 hours
- Pricing stable: Individual $129/year; Premium $180/year (bi-monthly scans + 60 custom removals); Family $329/year (4 people)
- Covers ~750 data brokers; manual, risk-prioritized approach; re-checks every 90 days
- No visible DROP-related marketing — second consecutive day of silence on what is their highest-profile competitive framing opportunity
- Efficacy gap vs Optery in independent testing continues to be cited in comparison content

## Privacy Bee

- No new announcements in the last 24–48 hours; blog last active in June 2026
- Remains the only major service with a **weekly monitoring cadence** (competitors are monthly or quarterly) and a Firefox browser extension
- Claims 1,000+ data broker contacts — highest stated broker count among established services
- Pricing: Essentials $8/month, Pro $18/month, Signature $67/month (all annual); premium tier is the most expensive in the market
- Strong positioning for enterprise and high-risk users; less competitive for budget-conscious individual consumers

## Kanary

- No new announcements in the last 24–48 hours
- Notable structural shift: **Kanary has pivoted to a mobile-first model** — rebranded primary product as "Kanary Copilot" (iOS only); web app still available for existing users; Android waitlist open
- Copilot takes a habit-building approach with bite-sized card-based privacy tasks (~10 minutes each), distinguishing it from "set it and forget it" competitors
- Only two platforms (Google and Twitch) have full settings-audit-and-fix automation in the app as of last confirmation; no new platform additions detected
- Pricing: $16.99/month or ~$179.88/year; 14-day free trial; 300+ brokers; claims 95–100% removal rate by day 90

## OneRep

- No new announcements today; most recent significant update was May 13, 2026
- **May 2026 expansion** (still being cited in new reviews): added 550+ non-public data brokers, custom removal requests, and breach monitoring to the Pro tier — meaningful product differentiation that continues to generate coverage
- Two-tier structure: standard plan (public brokers) and **OneRep Pro** (adds non-public brokers, breach alerts)
- Ongoing reputational liability: founder's ownership stake in Nuwber (a people-search data broker) continues to surface prominently in 2026 review content — largest trust deficit of any named competitor
- Efficacy remains below category average in independent tests (~42% removal rate vs Optery's ~68%)

## Aura

- **Notable security incident still generating coverage:** In March 2026, Aura disclosed that an unauthorized party accessed an employee account via voice phishing and exfiltrated approximately 900,000 records (names, home addresses, phone numbers, emails) — this is resurfacing in mid-2026 comparison and review content as a trust concern for a privacy-positioned product
- New feature: **AI Privacy Assistant** — analyzes personal online exposure and delivers personalized recommendations (old profiles, security settings, unprocessed data brokers); launched in 2026
- Expanded to 200+ data broker/people-search sites (up significantly from ~30 sites one year ago)
- Bundles data removal with identity monitoring, credit monitoring, VPN, and password management — differentiated as an all-in-one safety platform rather than a pure removal tool
- The March breach creates a clear messaging vulnerability for competitors

## Removaly

- No activity detected; service remains defunct (inactive since approximately 2022); appearing only in "alternatives to Removaly" comparison content

## Emerging Services

- **EasyOptOuts:** No new announcements; $19.99/year (market's lowest price for a functional service); 100+ sites; ~65% removal effectiveness in Consumer Reports testing — punches above its price point
- **CrabClear:** Continuing to appear in mainstream comparison roundups; claims 1,500+ broker coverage (highest stated count in the market); "EU-standard" privacy positioning; no formal funding or product announcement detected — continues to be a service to monitor
- **PrivacyOn:** Actively publishing content positioning itself against Removaly's defunct service to capture displaced users; no major product news

## Market Summary

Day 2 of California DROP enforcement finds every major competitor still silent on the biggest regulatory story in the industry's history, leaving the first-mover positioning window surprisingly open. The most significant new competitive risk today is Aura's March 2026 data breach continuing to resurface in review content — a trust vulnerability that pure-play removal services (Optery, Incogni, DeleteMe) should be exploiting in their messaging but are not.
