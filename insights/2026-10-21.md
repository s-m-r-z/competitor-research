# Competitor Insights — 2026-10-21

## DeleteMe
- No breaking news in the last 24–48 hours; service continues to operate with pricing at $129/year (1 person), $229/year (2 people), $329/year (4 people).
- Standard plan covers ~100 US-focused brokers; premium tiers claim 750+ brokers.
- Company marketing claims 141M+ opt-out removals completed — an unverified figure to watch for competitive positioning.
- ISO 27001:2022 certification continues to be cited as a trust differentiator in reviews; no new product announcements found.

## Incogni
- Unlimited plan (launched earlier in 2026) with Custom Removals is now a confirmed differentiator: privacy experts manually remove specific links submitted by users, beyond the 420+ automated broker scans.
- Custom Removals reportedly cover 3,000+ sites including social media, news sites, and non-standard brokers — a feature no other automated competitor currently matches at scale.
- Pricing holds at ~$6.49/month billed annually (lowest among major paid services); no new tier or pricing change found.
- No new announcements in the last 48 hours.

## Privacy Bee
- The August 12, 2026 acquisition of EraseMe.app continues to be the leading story; company is integrating EraseMe's automated removal technology into its platform; EraseMe customers awaiting transition instructions.
- Claims coverage of 1,128 data brokers (1,100+ automated), the highest broker count in the market.
- Perfect 5/5 PCMag Editors' Choice rating (August 5, 2026) remains the dominant third-party credibility signal.
- No new October 21 announcements found.

## Kanary
- No new product or pricing announcements found for October 21, 2026.
- Third-party reviews continue to note slow initial results (30–90 days) and conflicting pricing ($9.99–$16.99/month depending on source); prospective customers should verify directly.
- Niche "Kanary Copilot" app for streamers (Twitch/doxxing protection) remains a differentiator not replicated by competitors; no expansion news.

## OneRep
- No new activity found. Company remains weakened following the December 17, 2025 termination of its Mozilla Monitor Plus partnership over CEO Dimitri Shelest's ties to people-search businesses (Nuwber and others).
- Coverage estimated at ~235 people-search sites — below most major competitors.
- Former Mozilla Monitor Plus customers must re-engage data brokers independently.

## Optery
- Open-source data broker directory on GitHub (640+ brokers with opt-out links) continues to generate community goodwill and brand visibility.
- Consumer Reports independent testing (2024 benchmark, most recent available) ranked Optery Ultimate highest among paid services at 68% profile removal in four months.
- Pricing tiers range from free to $24.99/month; exact broker counts per tier vary widely across third-party sources.
- No new announcements in the last 48 hours.

## Removaly
- No significant news, press coverage, or product updates found. Service continues to have low visibility in recent rankings and comparison roundups.

## EasyOptOuts (Emerging)
- addy.io partnership (announced March 2026) remains active: addy.io Lite and Pro subscribers receive 25% lifetime discount for US customers.
- Service remains the lowest-cost option at $19.99/year with automated removal across 200+ brokers, rescanning every 4 months.
- Privacy Guides testing named it best value in the category; Consumer Reports results remain mixed — independent verification is limited.
- No new announcements found for October 21, 2026.

## Market Summary
No major competitor announcements were identified in the 24–48 hour window ending October 21, 2026. The competitive landscape remains defined by three macro forces: California's DROP platform (now in active enforcement since August 1, 2026) pressuring all paid services to differentiate beyond California-based brokers; Incogni's Custom Removals feature setting a new bar for manual/automated hybrid coverage; and Privacy Bee's EraseMe acquisition signaling consolidation at the premium end. EasyOptOuts continues to disrupt on price at $19.99/year while established players like OneRep and Removaly lose ground.
