## Market / Regulatory Developments
- **California DROP enforcement now active:** Since August 1, 2026, all registered California data brokers must access the state's DELETE Request and Opt-out Platform (DROP) at least once every 45 days and delete requests within 90 days. ~543 brokers registered; 300,000+ Californians had filed through it by spring 2026. Commercial services are framing DROP's monitoring gaps as their value-add.
- **Proposed registration fee increase:** California's CPPA has proposed raising the data broker registration fee to $6,600 per broker for the next cycle (unconfirmed if adopted).
- **Emerging competitor signal:** CrabClear (crabclear.com) appeared in multiple "best services" roundups dated 2026 — a newer entrant worth monitoring.
- **Aura** continues to be cited in comparison roundups; its data removal feature is bundled only inside a full identity protection plan starting at ~$12–15/month, not sold standalone.

## Market Summary
The data broker opt-out space is seeing no major product launches or pricing shifts this week; the dominant story is California's DROP platform forcing regulatory compliance while commercial services race to position around its gaps. Optery is gaining independent credibility through third-party test rankings, while Incogni is running aggressive Q4 discounting that could pressure mid-tier competitors on price.
