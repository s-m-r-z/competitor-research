# Competitor Insights — 2026-10-25

## DeleteMe
- International expansion appears stalled: as of late September 2026, DeleteMe's international enrollment page showed no available countries, suggesting the company has closed or paused signups outside the US.
- Independent testing (allaboutcookies.org, September 2026) found 33 sites with personal data, with 53 automated removal requests sent; 566 additional sites were flagged as "custom requests only," limiting actual automated coverage despite marketing claims of 750–976 brokers covered.
- A 2024 Consumer Reports study cited in recent reviews gave DeleteMe a 27% effectiveness score — the lowest among major services tested — which continues to surface in competitor comparison pieces.

## Incogni
- Incogni is running a 58% promotional discount on annual plans as of October 22, 2026, per Cybernews deal tracking — a notable price signal potentially aimed at acquisition or market-share growth.
- Gizmodo's October 12 four-month test reported 107 removal requests sent, 102 completed, with a privacy score of 54/100 (up from 24/100 baseline) — providing a credible independent data point for marketing benchmarks.
- The Deloitte limited-assurance audit (originally completed August 2025) continues to generate favorable coverage: CyberInsider's October 23 review highlights it as a trust differentiator, noting Incogni has processed 245M+ data removal requests with recurring requests sent every 60 days (public brokers) / 90 days (private brokers).
- Security.org's October 1 roundup named Incogni its top overall pick for 2026 among paid data removal services.

## Regulatory & Market Context
- **California DROP platform** (launched August 1, 2026): The state's free Delete Request and Opt-Out Platform has surpassed 300,000 sign-ups and logged ~450,000 consumer requests in its first weeks. However, the CPPA reported only ~30% of registered data brokers had begun processing requests in the first week — signaling significant compliance gaps and potential enforcement actions ahead. Brokers face penalties of $200/day/violation with no cure period.
- **Senate probe on opt-out page obstruction**: A May 2026 Senate investigation found Comscore, IQVIA Digital, Telesign, and 6sense Insights had coded their opt-out pages to block search engine indexing. Several companies are correcting this under pressure from Senator Maggie Hassan — broader industry scrutiny of dark patterns is intensifying.
- **Radaris disruption**: As of October 11, radaris.com shows a notice that the domain was transferred by court order, with no current opt-out route available — a notable people-search site exit from the landscape.

## Market Summary
The California DROP platform's rocky rollout (only 30% broker compliance in week one) is the most consequential near-term development, as enforcement actions could reshape which paid services offer genuine value over the free state tool. Incogni continues to dominate mindshare through its Deloitte audit credibility and aggressive discounting, while DeleteMe faces growing pressure from independent tests showing limited automated coverage depth.
