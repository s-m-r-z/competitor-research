# Competitor Insights — 2026-11-23

## DeleteMe
- Acquired social media privacy tool **Block Party** in March 2026, expanding into social media exposure protection and AI-driven targeting attacks; the two products continue to operate independently for now.
- Actively promoting California's DROP (Delete Request and Opt-Out Platform) as complementary to its own service; claims 141M+ removals processed to date.
- Covers 750+ data brokers per self-reporting; independent reviews note figures as low as ~70 sites with active removal, reflecting differences in how coverage is counted.
- Uses human reviewers for brokers requiring manual form submissions, fax, or notarized documentation — a differentiator from fully automated competitors.

## Incogni (Surfshark)
- Parent company Surfshark Group acquired a majority stake in **Optery** on September 7–8, 2026, adding a second data removal product to its portfolio alongside Incogni and Ironwall.
- Incogni Pro plans now cover 550+ non-public data brokers as of May 2026 (up from 420+ on standard plans).
- Incogni and Optery will remain separate brands and products; no cross-sell bundled with Surfshark VPN subscriptions announced.

## Optery
- Acquired by Surfshark Group (majority stake) in September 2026; will continue as a standalone business with its own team, product, and technology.
- Earned CNET "Editors' Choice" award for personal data removal in 2026.
- Covers 200+ data brokers; described by security.org as offering more detailed reports and better customer support than Incogni, though Incogni leads on international coverage.

## Kanary
- Published a **2026 Product Brief** with updates including SOC 2 Type 1 compliance achieved.
- Added LinkedIn and X (Twitter) monitoring for doxxing and impersonation to member plans.
- Reported that competitor Removaly is defunct; Kanary's blog attributes this to Removaly being taken offline (originally acquired by DuckDuckGo in 2023).

## Privacy Bee
- No major announcements found in the last 24–48 hours.
- Positioned as a premium, "set-and-forget" service at ~$197/year covering 350+ companies; reviews remain positive in 2026 roundups.

## Removaly

## Emerging / Notable Market Developments
- **CyberForget** appears in 2026 comparison articles alongside DeleteMe, Incogni, and Optery — a new entrant gaining visibility.
- **California DROP Platform**: As of November 5, 2026, California's privacy regulator confirmed the DROP system is performing stably at the 90-day compliance milestone. Over 500,000 Californians had submitted requests as of August 25. Data brokers must retrieve and process requests every 45 days or face $200/day/request penalties. This state-run tool represents a free alternative that could commoditize the core value proposition of paid removal services.
- **New state registries**: Connecticut and New Jersey enacted data broker registration laws in 2026, expanding the regulatory footprint.
- **EPIC report (May 2026)**: Found 8+ manipulative design patterns used by 38 data broker companies to obstruct opt-out requests — reinforces the value narrative for third-party removal services.
- **Senate report (Feb 2026)**: Covered brokers hiding opt-out pages from search engines; named companies subsequently removed the blocking code after the report's release.

## Market Summary
Consolidation is the defining trend of 2026: Surfshark now controls both Incogni and Optery under one group, while DeleteMe expanded into social media privacy via Block Party, signaling that standalone data broker removal is becoming a commodity layer within broader privacy bundles. California's free DROP platform poses an emerging structural threat to the entire category's pricing power, though the complexity of non-California brokers and manual removal processes continues to sustain demand for managed services.
