# Competitor Insights — 2026-08-09

## Privacy Bee
- **Major PR win (Aug 6, 2026):** Became the first and only data removal service to earn a perfect 5-star rating from PCMag — no service in the category had rated higher than 4.5 stars in PCMag's 44-year history. Named PCMag Editors' Choice, ranking ahead of Optery and Incogni (both 4.5 stars).
- Now covers 1,128 data brokers and 185,000+ supported custom sites, cited as "the most extensive data broker coverage on the market today."
- Continues to be positioned as a premium "always-on" monitoring and removal platform; featured recently in Travel + Leisure, Daily Mail, TechNewsWorld, and ZDNET.

## Incogni
- Latest comparison piece (updated Aug 5, 2026) pits Incogni vs. DeleteMe; Incogni is framed as the winner for automation breadth.
- Covers 420+ data broker sites; independently audited by Deloitte Lietuva UAB (Aug 2025 assurance report confirming 245M+ removal requests processed since Jan 2022).
- Pricing: ~$15.98/month or $95.88/year for Standard plan. Remains the go-to Surfshark-backed service for price-conscious consumers.

## Optery
- Published the **2026 Enterprise Social Engineering Survey Report** (May 2026) based on responses from 421 cybersecurity leaders — positions Optery as an enterprise-grade B2B player, not just a consumer tool.
- Expanded to **Canada** (excluding Quebec) in January 2026 — first major US competitor to enter the Canadian market with free Exposure Reports.
- Released a free, open-source directory of 640+ data brokers on GitHub (December 2025), building developer goodwill and SEO authority.
- Added **Map Blurring** feature for Extended/Ultimate subscribers — enables blurring of street-level home imagery on Google Maps and Apple Maps via the Optery dashboard.
- Recognized as Editors' Choice-winning privacy tool across multiple review outlets.

## DeleteMe
- Continues to hold position as the most established service; one roundup credits it with 3,610+ broker coverage, another with 122-site manual, risk-prioritized removal.
- Pricing: $129/year per person with family plans available.
- A Consumer Reports investigation rated DeleteMe as a "mid-level performer," with only 65%+ removal rates, trailing Optery and EasyOptOuts.
- No new product announcements or pricing changes detected in the last 48 hours.

## OneRep
- Pricing remains $99.99/year; covers 318 data brokers with automated monthly checks.
- Ongoing reputational risk: past affiliation with data broker operations continues to be cited in 2026 reviews, potentially limiting enterprise uptake.
- No new feature announcements found in the last 48 hours.

## Kanary
- Hybrid automated/manual approach with 95–100% removal rates claimed by day 90; covers 300+ data brokers.
- Pricing: $14.99–$16.99/month billed annually; family add-ons available.
- Strong enterprise positioning with monthly rescans and dedicated customer support.
- No new announcements in the last 48 hours.

## Removaly
- **Appears to have shut down.** Multiple 2026 sources now reference Removaly in the past tense and direct users to alternatives. No active service detected.

## Emerging Players
- **PrivacyOn** is being cited as "Editor's Choice for 2026" in at least one aggregator roundup, combining competitive broker coverage with aggressive pricing — worth monitoring as a new entrant gaining review traction.
- **EasyOptOuts** continues to surface in Consumer Reports testing as a top performer (65%+ removal rate), suggesting strong organic growth despite lower brand awareness.

## Regulatory Development: California DROP Goes Live
- **August 1, 2026:** California's Delete Request and Opt-Out Platform (DROP), mandated by the Delete Act, went live for broker-side processing.
- Over 600 registered data brokers must now access DROP at least every 45 days and process deletion requests within 90 days. Penalties: up to $200/day per violation.
- Hundreds of thousands of Californians have already signed up. This is the most significant US privacy regulation to take effect this year and directly competes with the value proposition of paid opt-out services.

## Market Summary
Privacy Bee's unprecedented PCMag 5-star rating and California's DROP platform launch are the two headline developments this week — Privacy Bee gains a significant brand credibility edge, while DROP's activation may commoditize basic opt-out services for California residents and pressure the entire industry to differentiate on breadth, speed, and non-California coverage. Removaly's apparent shutdown signals continued consolidation among smaller players.
