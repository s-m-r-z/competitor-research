# Competitor Insights — 2026-08-05

## Regulatory Environment (Critical — Affects All Players)
- California's DELETE Act DROP (Delete Request and Opt-Out Platform) enforcement went live **August 1, 2026** — data brokers must now access DROP at least once every 45 days and process verified deletion requests, with fines of $200 per violation per day
- Any California resident can now submit a single deletion request to all registered data brokers at once via DROP — this could reduce organic demand for paid removal services among privacy-aware California users
- New Jersey enacted its own data broker law on June 30, 2026, targeting both data brokers and "data collectors" that supply them — the first law to regulate both sides of the data supply chain
- Expanding federal and multi-state regulatory momentum projected through 2027

## Emerging / Adjacent Players

## Market Summary
California's DROP enforcement going live on August 1 is the most consequential near-term market event — it adds regulatory legitimacy to the category and may shift consumer behavior, while also pressuring data brokers to comply at scale for the first time. Optery and Incogni are gaining the most ground through awards, audits, and aggressive promotional activity, while Removaly's shutdown creates an immediate pool of displaced customers that all competitors will be targeting.
