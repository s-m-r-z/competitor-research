# Competitor Insights — 2026-11-18

## DeleteMe
- International enrollment has closed as of September 2026; no countries are listed for international plans, leaving the US-only plan ($129/year) as the sole offering
- Site list (dated September 17, 2026) names 969 brokers; some third-party reviews list a much lower figure (~100 sites), suggesting inconsistent coverage reporting
- Published regulatory briefings on California's DROP platform, noting the August 1, 2026 enforcement start requiring brokers to process requests every 45 days
- Received All About Cookies 2026 Editors' Choice recognition (awarded February 2026)

## Privacy Bee
- Earned PCMag Editors' Choice with a perfect 5/5 star rating, announced August 6, 2026 — the first and only data removal service to achieve that score from PCMag
- Claims coverage of 1,128 data brokers and 185,000+ supported custom sites as of the announcement
- No new product updates or pricing changes detected in the last 48 hours

## Kanary
- Remains active; covers 300+ data broker and people-search sites
- An academic study (~9 months old) rated Kanary the worst performer despite its wide coverage, conflicting with current 2026 review roundups that rank it favorably
- No pricing changes or feature announcements detected recently

## Removaly
- **Effectively defunct** — confirmed by an academic paper and Kanary's own blog: Removaly was taken offline with only a vague message from the team; it is no longer available to new or existing customers

## OneRep
- Narrower focus on people-search sites (~200+ sites); positioned in comparison articles as a smaller, more targeted alternative to broader services
- No recent announcements or pricing changes detected

## Incogni
- Pricing holding at ~$7.99/month ($95.88/year) billed annually; 420+ data broker sites covered
- Custom Removals feature (human-assisted takedowns of sites outside the automated list) introduced in 2025 continues to differentiate the service
- Published comparison content (Incogni vs. Optery) positioning themselves favorably against direct competitors

## Optery
- Tiered pricing: free scan, Core $39/year, Extended $149/year, Ultimate (620+ automated + 935+ with custom requests)
- Runs ongoing monthly rescans, more frequent than some competitors' quarterly cadence
- No new feature launches or pricing changes detected in the last 48 hours

## Emerging Competitors

### ShieldApps Privacy *(New Launch — November 13, 2026)*
- **ShieldApps**, a cybersecurity software company, officially launched **ShieldApps Privacy**, a new data removal platform combining: personal data removal, dark web monitoring, antivirus protection, and VPN technology
- Available for individuals, families, and businesses at https://privacy.shieldapps.com/
- Notable as the first all-in-one cybersecurity + data removal bundle from a known security software vendor — may target less privacy-focused consumers already using security suites

## Regulatory & Market Context
- **California DROP platform**: 300,000+ Californians have enrolled since launch; brokers required to process requests every 45 days starting August 1, 2026 — creates a free public alternative that may commoditize paid services for California residents
- **Dutch DPA enforcement**: The Netherlands' data protection regulator has announced it will focus enforcement specifically on data brokers, increasing compliance pressure across the industry
- **EU GDPR simplification**: The EU Council agreed a negotiating position that would treat pseudonymized data as non-personal for recipients who cannot re-identify individuals — could affect broker liability frameworks
- **General Motors privacy settlement**: Multistate settlement adding heavier obligations for driver data privacy signals growing regulatory scrutiny on data collection broadly

## Market Summary
The data broker opt-out market continues to consolidate — Removaly's shutdown leaves one less mid-tier competitor — while ShieldApps Privacy's November 13 launch signals that established cybersecurity vendors are entering the space by bundling data removal with security suites. California's free DROP platform reaching 300K+ users is the most significant structural pressure on paid services, particularly affecting lower-tier offerings that compete primarily on convenience.
