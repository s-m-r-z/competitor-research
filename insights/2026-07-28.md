# Competitor Insights — 2026-07-28

## DeleteMe
- Maintains its position as the most established brand in the space (operating since 2011), using a hybrid human-researcher + automation approach for ~$129/year.
- Coverage remains around 85–100 data brokers on the standard plan — significantly lower than Incogni (420+) and Optery (270–630+), which continues to draw unfavorable comparisons in 2026 review roundups.
- Rated a "mid-level performer" in Consumer Reports' 2024 investigation; no new 2026 awards or recognition found.
- Recommended as top pick for users outside California who prefer human-assisted opt-outs over pure automation.

## Incogni
- Holding strong as the leading budget-friendly automated service at $6.49–$7.99/month (billed annually), now covering 420+ data brokers across US and Europe.
- Continues to benefit from an independent Deloitte audit verifying its removal practices — a rare and significant trust differentiator in the category.
- Received PCMag and PCWorld Editors' Choice recognition in 2026; remains top-ranked on Cybernews, AllAboutCookies, and TechRadar.
- No longer requires power of attorney from users; re-sends removal requests every 60–90 days automatically.
- Parent company Nord Security raised a $100M financing round (led by Novator Ventures, with General Catalyst and Burda Principal Investments) — signals continued investment and likely product expansion ahead.
- No new standalone product announcements in the last 48 hours.

## Emerging Services & Market Entrants
- **EasyOptOuts**: Budget outlier at $19.99/year; covers ~160 brokers (primarily people-search sites); sends opt-out requests and reports 3x per year (every 4 months). Gaining mentions in budget-focused roundups.
- **HelloPrivacy**: Now operating as "Privacy Protect by Array"; moved to daily scans. Covers 100+ sites; dark web monitoring included.

## Regulatory / Market Developments (High Importance)
- **California DROP platform goes live August 1, 2026**: Data brokers must begin processing deletion requests from California's free Drop Request and Opt-out Platform (DROP) starting August 1. Brokers must access DROP every 45 days, delete all associated personal data (including inferences), and report status — or face fines of $200 per request per day of noncompliance. This is the single biggest market event this week. It could reduce paid-service uptake from California-based users or shift the value proposition of commercial services toward non-CA residents and more complex use cases.
- **FTC banned location data sales (July 2026)**: Per DataGrail's July 2026 Privacy Dispatch, the FTC issued a ban on location data sales, though with implementation caveats ("with a catch"). Details forthcoming in regulatory filings.
- **State enforcement tightening**: As of mid-July 2026, cure periods for privacy law violations have expired in nine states — first violations now trigger direct enforcement, not warnings. Increases compliance pressure on data brokers and may indirectly expand the TAM for opt-out services.
- **Industry consolidation upstream**: Publicis acquired LiveRamp in 2026; Omnicom acquired Acxiom's parent company in 2025. Consumer data is concentrating into fewer ad conglomerates, potentially making opt-out targeting more complex and strengthening the case for professional removal services.

---

## Market Summary

The most significant event in the data broker opt-out space this week is California's DROP platform enforcement deadline of August 1, 2026 — the first government-run universal deletion mechanism of its scale — which will pressure commercial services to better articulate their value beyond what DROP offers. Meanwhile, Optery's CNET Best Overall 2026 win and Incogni's $100M parent-company raise signal that the premium and budget ends of the market are both gaining ground, while mid-tier players like Removaly are showing signs of fading from visibility.
