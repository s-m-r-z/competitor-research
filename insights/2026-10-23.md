# Competitor Insights — 2026-10-23

## Incogni
- **Concierge tier launched October 14, 2026** — a managed, human-assisted privacy plan priced at $4,999/yr for up to five adults. Features a dedicated privacy specialist conducting weekly manual scans, custom removal requests beyond broker databases, emergency support from former law enforcement officers, dark web monitoring, VPN, password manager, private email alias, and a secure phone number. Identity-theft recovery reported at up to $1M per person (via Coveron).
- Dashboard updated with new Exposures, Broker Overview, and Privacy Score tabs (score now visible on Unlimited and Protect plans; Gizmodo recorded 54/100 in its test).
- Gizmodo's 4-month test (published Oct 12) recorded 107 removal requests, 102 completed; automated removals resolved in 2–15 days, one custom removal took 91 days.
- Current pricing: Standard at $7.99/mo ($95.88/yr billed annually); Unlimited at $14.99/mo; Family at $191.88/yr; Protect (Unlimited + Coveron) at $20.74/mo.

## Privacy Bee
- In August 2026, PCMag awarded Privacy Bee a perfect 5-star Editors' Choice rating — the first time PCMag has given a perfect score in this category. Optery and Incogni were rated 4.5 at the same time.
- Privacy Bee acquired EraseMe.app on August 12, 2026, absorbing its customer base and technology. Existing Privacy Bee subscribers are unaffected.
- Coverage now stands at 1,128 data brokers and 185,000+ supported custom sites as of August 2026.
- ZDNET named it best data removal service for enhanced protection in its April 2026 roundup.

## DeleteMe
- International enrollment closed as of September 21, 2026 — the international plans page no longer lists available countries, effectively shutting out non-US signups. (Reported by a competitor source; not yet confirmed by DeleteMe directly.)
- Pricing remains $129/yr (1 person), $229/yr (2 people), $329/yr (family of 4). Broker coverage claimed at 750+, though independent verification is limited.
- A 2024 Consumer Reports test (small sample) gave DeleteMe a 27% removal rate — the weakest result among tested services; no updated independent test found for 2026.

## Optery
- Published a case study on October 12, 2026 showing a large consumer technology company expanded Optery's employee data removal program from ~20 executives to hundreds of staff across multiple functions — 53,326 exposed profiles removed and 2.24 million scans completed for participants.
- Secured a reseller partnership with GuidePoint Security (announced September 29, 2026).
- Holds CNET and PCMag Editors' Choice designations for 2026. Optery's own statement claims it ranked #1 in a Consumer Reports people-search removal study (study tested 13 brokers).
- Pricing tiers: Free, $39, $149, $249/yr.

## OneRep

## Kanary

## Regulatory & Market Context
- **California AB 883** signed by Governor Newsom: shortens the DELETE Request and Opt-Out Platform (DROP) processing cycle from every 45 days to every 30 days. DROP launched January 2026; enforcement began August 1, 2026. More than 600 data brokers are now registered with the state.
- An investigation found 35 of 499 registered data brokers have added `noindex` metadata to their opt-out and deletion pages, making those pages harder for consumers to find.
- A new study categorized the four major paid removal services as "relatively ineffective" as a group, citing large result gaps between users of the same service.

## Market Summary
October 2026 is defined by Incogni's aggressive move upmarket with a $4,999/yr Concierge tier, while Privacy Bee holds its PCMag 5-star lead and DeleteMe faces headwinds from its international pullback and a low independent test score. On the regulatory front, California's DROP cycle tightening and growing awareness of broker opt-out obstruction are accelerating consumer demand — a rising tide that benefits the whole sector but raises the bar on demonstrated removal effectiveness.
