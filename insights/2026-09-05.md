# Competitor Insights — 2026-09-05

## DeleteMe
- No new announcements or product changes in the last 24–48 hours
- Continues to be classified as a "mid-level performer" per Consumer Reports; mixed results in independent testing persist as a reputation drag
- Still the primary recommendation for users outside California where DROP is unavailable; positioned as the "most established" service (122+ broker sites, human-led quarterly rechecks)
- Pricing unchanged: $129/year (1 person), $180/year Premium, $329/year (4-person family); no free or true monthly tier

## Incogni (Surfshark)
- September 2026 "Data Removal Awareness" promotion continues: 55% off annual plans (~$7.99/month effective) — entering day 2+ of the campaign; potential for meaningful subscriber acquisition pressure on competitors
- Recently published "Gen AI and LLM Data Privacy Ranking 2026" on their blog — signals expansion of content strategy into AI/privacy intersection, a timely topic likely to drive organic traffic and media pickup
- Core differentiators remain: Deloitte third-party audit, SOC 2 Type 2 certification, 2,420+ broker coverage across premium tier
- Continues to hold top-ranked overall service position in independent comparisons (Security.org, AllAboutCookies)

## Kanary

- Pricing per current listings: $9.99/month; discrepancy between some third-party listings (still showing $14.99–$16.99) and confirmed current tiers may still be causing consumer confusion

- No new product or pricing announcements in the last 24–48 hours

## Privacy Bee

- EraseMe.app integration: no new public timeline update; customers migrating to Privacy Bee's monitoring and removal platform
- Coverage: 885+ data brokers; $197/year (single plan, no tiers); no free tier remains a friction point compared to Optery
- Most extensive broker coverage claim on the market per PCMag review

## Regulatory & Market Context
- California DROP platform continues to be the dominant regulatory story: operational since August 1, 2026 (mandatory 45-day processing cycle); 345,000+ requests processed as of August 7 board meeting
- CPPA Data Broker Strike Force enforcement actively ongoing; rising registration fees ($6,000 in 2026 → $9,500 in 2027) will pressure smaller brokers, potentially shrinking the broker universe automated services need to cover
- General market dynamic: data reappears within 3–6 months of removal for most consumers, keeping the subscription model structurally defensible across all competitors

## Market Summary
Today's standout competitive movement is Optery's CNET "Best Overall Data Removal Service of 2026" award gaining wider circulation — giving Optery a strong head-to-head credibility claim against Privacy Bee's newly earned PCMag 5-star crown — while Incogni's 55% September promotional discount continues to apply subscriber-acquisition pressure across the mid-market; no major product launches were detected in the last 24–48 hours, suggesting a relatively quiet news cycle with underlying tensions around award positioning and pricing.
