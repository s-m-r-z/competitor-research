# Competitor Insights — 2026-11-07

## DeleteMe
- No major product announcements in the last 24–48 hours; latest coverage consists of review and comparison articles.
- Coverage claims vary widely across sources: internal marketing cites 750+ broker sites scanned, with active removals from ~170 of the most prominent brokers.
- Pricing remains at $129/year for individuals, $229/year for 2-person family plan.
- Re-listing continues to be a commonly cited weakness — brokers re-acquire data after opt-outs, requiring ongoing subscription to maintain coverage.
- An independent 2024 Consumer Reports study (still being referenced in 2026 reviews) found DeleteMe removed 27% of results from 13 people-search sites within four months.

## Incogni (Surfshark)
- No specific announcements in the past 24–48 hours.
- A new **Incogni Protect** bundle plan reported in 2026 combines data removal with identity theft restoration, insurance, and dark web monitoring. Pricing listed as: Basic ~$6.49/mo, Pro ~$9.49/mo, Family ~$12.49/mo.
- Coverage is disputed: vendor claims 420+ brokers; independent comparisons put the count closer to 180.
- Reviewers note the "blind opt-out" method means Incogni cannot confirm what specific data was found or removed.
- **Strategic context:** Incogni's parent company Surfshark acquired a majority stake in Optery in September 2026 (see Optery section), consolidating two of the top-rated services under one corporate umbrella.

## Optery
- **Major corporate development:** Surfshark Group acquired a majority stake in Optery, announced September 7–10, 2026. Financial terms were not disclosed.
- Optery continues operating as a standalone company with same C-level executives and core team — no immediate customer-facing changes.
- This is Surfshark's second privacy-services acquisition, following Ironwall in 2024, signaling an aggressive consolidation strategy.
- **Awards:** CNET named Optery its best overall data removal service of 2026; PCMag Editors' Choice for five consecutive years.
- **Growth:** Ranked No. 177 on the 2026 Inc. 5000 list.
- **New partnerships:** GuidePoint Security (Sept 29) and Carahsoft Technology (Sept 23) partnerships reported, suggesting enterprise and government channel expansion.
- Coverage reportedly expands to 560+ broker sites across plans.

## OneRep
- **Product expansion:** On May 6, 2026, OneRep published a blog post titled "OneRep expands coverage to non-public data brokers" — marking a notable broadening beyond the public people-search sites the service was originally known for. Details were not fully surfaced in search results.
- Coverage is vendor-claimed at 310+ brokers; scan cadence is monthly (more frequent than DeleteMe's quarterly scans).
- Pricing ranges from $14.95 to $29.95/month depending on plan.
- OneRep verifies that a person's data exists on a site before sending removal requests, in contrast to Incogni's "blind" approach.

## Privacy Bee
- No announcements in the last 24–48 hours; latest coverage is review/comparison articles.
- PCMag reportedly awarded Privacy Bee the first-ever perfect 5-star rating among data removal services (date of article not confirmed).
- Coverage is debated: some comparisons call it less comprehensive than dedicated removal services; others argue it covers a broader set of sites.
- No reported pricing changes or product launches.

## Kanary
- No announcements in the last 24–48 hours.
- Current coverage positioned at 300+ brokers with continuous monitoring and robust security features per a 2026 TechRadar review.
- CNET reviewed Kanary as a data removal service "aimed at tackling serious modern issues," positioning it in the security-aware consumer segment.
- Two data brokers — GladIKnow and Spytox — went dark according to a Kanary blog post, reducing total addressable broker counts industrywide.

## Removaly
- **Defunct.** Removaly has ceased operations following an acquisition. The company is no longer in business. Existing customers should be aware that the service is no longer active.

## EasyOptOuts (Emerging/Budget)
- Budget competitor at ~$19.99/year. TechRadar reviewed it in May 2026 as scanning 200+ US-centric people-finder sites with fully automated requests.
- A 2024 Consumer Reports study (still cited in 2026) found it left 35% of profiles intact — an effectiveness gap noted in competitor-authored reviews.

## CyberForget (New Entrant)
- Positioned as a new entrant offering automated re-removal when brokers re-list data. Pricing claimed at $19.99/month or $99/year.
- Currently lacks independent third-party reviews; primary source is the vendor's own comparison content.

## California DROP Platform (Regulatory)
- California's Delete Request and Opt-Out Platform (DROP) launched January 1, 2026, allowing state residents to file a single deletion request covering 665+ registered data brokers.
- Brokers must pass deletion requests on to their contractors and service providers.
- This government-run free tool represents structural competitive pressure on paid removal services, particularly for California residents.
- A March 2026 report found some brokers had hidden opt-out pages from Google; reportedly corrected since.

## Market Summary
The data broker opt-out market is consolidating rapidly: Surfshark's September 2026 acquisition of Optery — combined with its existing Incogni service — creates the dominant privacy-services conglomerate, while Removaly's closure reduces the competitive field. The California DROP platform's expansion to 665+ brokers is the most significant external threat to paid services, raising questions about differentiation and value proposition for consumers in regulated states.
