# Competitor Insights — 2026-09-03

## Kanary
- **Significant pricing tier restructure confirmed**: Core plan now $25/month ($250/year); Advanced plan now $50/month ($500/year) — a notable jump from the previously tracked $14.99–$16.99/month range; positions Kanary well above entry-level automated competitors like Incogni but frames the premium as analyst-backed removal
- **X (Twitter) doxxing detection launched**: Kanary now integrates with X to detect doxxing, personal data leaks, and impersonation; Advanced members receive automatic scans with actionable removal steps — differentiated feature not offered by DeleteMe, Incogni, or Optery
- **LinkedIn coverage added**: Advanced tier now monitors LinkedIn exposure, adding a high-value professional-data vector that most competitors haven't addressed
- **Scale milestone**: 2026 product brief (published July 2026) reports Kanary has monitored or mitigated exposure across 68,000 unique sites — significant breadth claim vs. Incogni's 420+ broker list and DeleteMe's 122 sites

## DeleteMe
- No new announcements or pricing changes in the last 24–48 hours
- Continues manual, risk-prioritized approach covering 122 sites; no indication of a response to Kanary's social-media monitoring features or Incogni's bundle strategy

- No new announcements in the last 24–48 hours

- SOC 2 Type 2 certification remains a differentiator in B2B/enterprise sales conversations

## OneRep

## Optery
- No new announcements in the last 24–48 hours

## Removaly / DuckDuckGo Privacy Pro
- Clarification from prior reporting: Removaly was acquired by DuckDuckGo in 2023 and fully integrated into **DuckDuckGo Privacy Pro** — it did not simply "shut down"; DuckDuckGo Privacy Pro now competes in the data removal space with Removaly's technology under a major brand umbrella, which is an important competitive dynamic underreported in head-to-head comparisons of the named players

## California DROP Enforcement — Ongoing Updates
- **First enforcement actions filed**: CPPA announced action against **LocateSmarter, LLC** (August 11) and **Cybba, Inc.** (August 13) for failure to register as data brokers — the first enforcement shots under the Delete Act
- **DROP penalties confirmed**: $200 per unprocessed request per day — high enough to generate urgency among non-compliant brokers and continued marketing opportunities for opt-out services
- **Formal rulemaking for DROP compliance audits approved** at August CPPA board meeting — expanded oversight infrastructure is coming
- **2027 registration fee approved at $9,500** (up from $6,000 in 2026) — rising compliance costs will pressure smaller brokers and could accelerate voluntary broker exits, shrinking the universe of brokers that automated services need to cover

## Market Summary
Kanary's previously unreported pricing restructure (Core now $25/month, Advanced $50/month) and social-media monitoring features (X doxxing detection, LinkedIn coverage) represent the most meaningful competitive developments of the past 24–48 hours, repositioning Kanary as a premium, analyst-backed service rather than a mid-market alternative. Meanwhile, California DROP's first formal enforcement actions signal that regulatory pressure is now operational, giving the entire sector fresh urgency-driven marketing material — and Incogni and Optery, with their automated scale, are best positioned to capitalize.
