# Competitor Insights — 2026-09-26

## Optery
- **Acquired by Surfshark** (announced September 7, 2026): Surfshark Group acquired Optery, adding it to a privacy portfolio that already includes Incogni and Ironwall. Optery will continue operating independently with its own team, product, and technology, but the two companies will share expertise around data-broker coverage and new privacy tools.
- **Carahsoft Partnership** (announced September 23, 2026): Optery partnered with Carahsoft Technology Corp. to bring its personal data removal platform to the public sector, expanding its addressable market to government agencies.
- **CNET Best Overall Award**: CNET named Optery Editors' Choice and the Best Overall Data Removal Service of 2026, ranking it first among leading data removal services.
- Ranked No. 177 on the 2026 Inc. 5000 List of America's Fastest-Growing Private Companies.

## Privacy Bee
- **PCMag Perfect 5-Star Rating** (announced August 5–6, 2026): Privacy Bee became the first and only data removal service to receive a perfect 5/5 from PCMag, earning Editors' Choice. No competitor had rated higher than 4.5 stars across PCMag's 44-year history of the category.
- **Acquired EraseMe.app**: Privacy Bee completed acquisition of EraseMe.app, a personal data removal tool. EraseMe.app customers will migrate to Privacy Bee's platform; the technology will be folded into Privacy Bee's product.
- Now covers **1,128 data brokers** and more than 185,000 custom supported sites, making it the broadest coverage claim in the market.

## Incogni
- September 17–18, 2026 reviews confirm Incogni covers **420+ brokers** and is backed by a Deloitte-audited process, starting at $7.99/month.
- A September 18, 2026 head-to-head analysis named Incogni the best overall value for automated recurring removals.
- Customer support team actively responding to Trustpilot reviews as recently as September 21–23, 2026.
- Now part of Surfshark Group alongside the newly acquired Optery, raising questions about potential consolidation or feature overlap.

## DeleteMe
- No major product announcements in the last 48 hours; service continues standard 90-day re-check cadence.
- California's DROP (Delete Request and Opt-out Platform), which went live January 1, 2026 and became mandatory for data brokers as of August 1, 2026, is the biggest external force affecting DeleteMe's value proposition for California residents—a free government alternative now handles in-state removals.
- DeleteMe continues to position itself as the go-to solution for users outside California.

## Kanary
- **LinkedIn coverage added** (July 2026): Advanced tier members can now monitor and request removal of personal data from LinkedIn directly through Kanary.
- **X (Twitter) integration** (June 2026): Kanary added scanning for doxxing, personal data leaks, and impersonation on X, with automatic scans and actionable removal steps for Advanced members.
- Expanded from mobile-only sign-ups to full web platform availability, widening the top-of-funnel.
- Covers 300+ data brokers; pricing from $9.99/month (standard) to $16.99/month (monthly Advanced) or $14.99/month (annual Advanced).

## OneRep
- **Non-public broker expansion** (May 2026): OneRep expanded coverage to more than 550 non-public (B2B) data brokers, moving beyond people-search sites to address behind-the-scenes data circulation.
- Ongoing reputational risk: Multiple reviewers continue flagging that OneRep's CEO founded numerous people-search sites—the same category of sites the service charges to remove data from. No public response from the company on this issue.

## Removaly
- Removaly appears to have ceased active operations around early 2022 and is no longer a meaningful competitor; multiple 2026 sources recommend alternatives. No new activity detected.

## Market Summary
The data broker opt-out industry is in a rapid consolidation phase—Surfshark's acquisition of Optery (following its earlier ownership of Incogni) concentrates significant market share under one parent company, while Privacy Bee's first-ever perfect PCMag rating and aggressive acquisition of EraseMe.app signal an increasingly competitive premium tier. California's free DROP platform adds meaningful regulatory tailwind that all paid services must now differentiate against.
