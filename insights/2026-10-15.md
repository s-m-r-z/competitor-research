# Competitor Insights — 2026-10-15

## DeleteMe
- October 2026 Incognito newsletter highlights service coverage of 750+ data brokers and announces a new joint report on AI and social engineering with 451 Research.
- Broker count remains contested: All About Cookies' October 12 analysis found DeleteMe's claimed 976 sites includes ~566 custom-request-only entries and 170 international-plan-only brokers, inflating the headline number.
- Pricing spreads across sources: ~$6.97/month (All About Cookies) to ~$10.75/month (Security Hero) — no official price change confirmed.
- DeleteMe newsletter also covered Radaris losing 14 domains (including radaris.com) after a New Jersey court ruling for repeatedly evading state privacy law; Radaris is appealing.

## Privacy Bee
- Acquired EraseMe.app in August 2026, folding its data-removal technology and customer base into Privacy Bee's platform; existing EraseMe.app visitors now redirect to Privacy Bee. Financial terms undisclosed.
- Earned PCMag's first-ever perfect 5-star rating (Editors' Choice) for a data removal service, announced August 6, 2026.
- Claims 1,128 data brokers and 185,000+ supported custom sites as of August 2026 (company-sourced figures).
- Pricing: approximately $96–$804/year depending on tier.

## Kanary
- No major announcements in the past 48 hours; continues to be active and well-regarded.
- TechRadar's 2026 review names Kanary a top US data removal service; PCMag rates it 4.0 stars.
- Published a blog post explaining Removaly's history (DuckDuckGo acquired Removaly in 2023 and uses it to power DuckDuckGo Privacy Pro's data broker removal feature; Removaly as a standalone consumer service is no longer active).

## Removaly
- No longer an active standalone service; was acquired by DuckDuckGo in 2023 and now exclusively powers DuckDuckGo Privacy Pro's data broker removal feature.

## OneRep
- No new product announcements found in the past 48 hours.
- Ongoing reputation concern in the industry: multiple review sources (Security.org, Incogni's blog) continue flagging the company's leadership ties to people-search site operators as a conflict of interest.
- Re-scan cadence is monthly, faster than Incogni's 60–90-day cycle.

## Incogni
- No new announcements in the past 48 hours.
- Remains a top-ranked service in All About Cookies' October 12 update, alongside DeleteMe and Aura.
- Owns competitor comparison content targeting OneRep (conflict-of-interest narrative) and Optery.
- Pricing: ~$94/year standalone; ~$78/year bundled with Surfshark.
- Deloitte issued a limited-assurance audit of Incogni's removal claims in August 2025 — a credibility differentiator the service continues to market.
- **Strategic note:** Surfshark (Incogni's parent) acquired a majority stake in Optery in early September 2026, making Surfshark the umbrella owner of both Incogni and Optery — two of the highest-profile removal services in the market.

## Optery
- **Major development (September 2026):** Surfshark acquired a majority stake in Optery. Optery will remain operationally independent with its own team, product, and technology. Surfshark's other brands (Incogni, Ironwall, HeyPolo) remain separate, but the companies plan to share knowledge and capabilities.
- This makes Surfshark Group the most consolidated player in the data removal space, now controlling both Incogni and Optery.
- Optery currently serves US and Canada only, with international expansion signaled.
- Pricing: ~$240–$1,188/year depending on tier; Consumer Reports' field test named Optery's top tier the highest-scoring paid service.

## DuckDuckGo Privacy Pro
- Bundles VPN, identity theft remediation, and data broker removal (powered by Removaly's acquired technology) into a single subscription.
- Growing as a bundled privacy offering, positioned outside the standalone removal service category.

## Regulatory / Market
- **California DELETE Act / DROP platform:** CalPrivacy announced the first-ever "Delete Week" on October 12, 2026, to highlight consumer data broker protections. As of mid-October, 560,000+ Californians have registered on the DROP platform.
- Data broker deletion obligations (brokers must process DROP requests) took effect August 1, 2026; brokers must check the platform at least every 45 days. More than 325,000 deletion requests have been submitted to 600+ registered brokers since January 2026.
- Key limitation noted by multiple sources: DROP does not monitor whether deleted data stays off — it relies on broker self-reporting, creating a market opportunity for paid monitoring services.
- Penalties for non-compliance: up to $200/day per unprocessed request, with no cure period.

## Market Summary
The data removal space is consolidating rapidly: Surfshark Group now controls both Incogni and Optery, while Privacy Bee absorbed EraseMe.app — meaning the top three or four rated services are increasingly under fewer ownership groups. California's DROP platform milestone (560K+ registered users, "Delete Week" Oct 12) is generating consumer awareness that may drive new sign-ups across paid services, particularly given DROP's self-reported-only enforcement gap.
