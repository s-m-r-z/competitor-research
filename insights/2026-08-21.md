# Competitor Insights — 2026-08-21

## Privacy Bee
- **Major milestone**: Privacy Bee became the first and only data removal service to earn a perfect 5-star (5.0 "Exemplary") rating from PCMag, announced August 6, 2026 — a first in PCMag's 44-year history of testing in this category.
- PCMag credited Privacy Bee with "the most extensive data broker coverage on the market today" and "the most comprehensive service for protecting your personal info from data aggregators."
- Current PCMag rankings (as of August 5, 2026): Privacy Bee 5.0 → Optery 4.5 → Incogni 4.5 → Kanary 4.0 → DeleteMe 3.5.
- The 5-star review is a significant marketing differentiator and is likely driving increased consumer trust and conversions for Privacy Bee.

## DeleteMe
- Holds roughly 750-broker coverage with re-checks every 90 days; still a leading brand but rated 3.5/5 by PCMag — below several newer competitors.
- Pricing remains at $129/year per person with family plans; no new pricing or feature announcements noted in the last 48 hours.
- Identified as a "mid-level performer" in earlier Consumer Reports testing; the PCMag rating gap vs. Privacy Bee could pressure DeleteMe to respond with feature/pricing updates.

## Incogni
- Rated 4.5/5 by PCMag; covers 420+ data brokers with automatic re-submissions when data reappears.
- Pricing: $6.49/month billed annually ($77.88/year), making it one of the more price-competitive options.
- No major product or pricing announcements found in the last 48 hours.

## OneRep
- In May 2026, OneRep expanded coverage to 550+ non-public data brokers, added custom removal requests for sites outside its standard list, and launched data breach monitoring alerts.
- Ongoing reputational concern: the CEO has previously founded people-search sites, raising trust questions that continue to circulate in review coverage.
- No new announcements found in the last 48 hours.

## Optery
- Rated 4.5/5 by PCMag; offers "Expanded Reach" feature covering 250+ additional data brokers beyond its core list.
- US-headquartered with strong coverage of US-specific people-search sites.
- No pricing changes or new features announced in the last 48 hours.

## Kanary
- Rated 4.0/5 by PCMag; boasts a >90% removal success rate within 14 days on covered sites.
- No major new announcements found in the last 48 hours.

## Removaly
- **Shut down**: Removaly has ceased operations. Searches confirm it is no longer available, with guides now pointing to Removaly alternatives. This may create market opportunity to capture former Removaly customers.

## Regulatory / Market — California DROP Platform
- **August 1, 2026 enforcement began**: California's Delete Request and Opt-Out Platform (DROP) now mandates that registered data brokers process deletion requests retrieved from the platform at least every 45 days and finalize deletions within 90 days. Penalty for non-compliance: $200 per request per day.
- Over 450,000 Californians have submitted DROP requests; 600+ data brokers are registered.
- The CPPA has launched a **Data Broker Strike Force** to enforce the Delete Act amid this crackdown.
- CPPA is advancing a proposal to raise the annual data broker registration fee from $6,000 to $9,500 starting 2027, and to require third-party audits every 3 years starting 2028.
- The enforcement deadline creates urgency for consumers and may increase demand for automated opt-out services that navigate the new compliance landscape.

## Market Summary
Privacy Bee's landmark PCMag 5-star rating is the standout competitive event of the week, positioning it as the new premium standard in the data removal space and putting pressure on DeleteMe and others to respond. Meanwhile, California's DROP enforcement going live on August 1 has materially raised the stakes for data brokers and may further accelerate consumer adoption of opt-out services across the board.
