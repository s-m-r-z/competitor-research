# Competitor Insights — 2026-11-15

## DeleteMe
- No new announcements or pricing changes detected in the last 24–48 hours.
- Pricing remains at $129/year (Individual); continues positioning California's DROP platform as complementary rather than a free substitute in its October 2026 blog post.
- Coverage claims conflict: DeleteMe self-reports 750+ brokers; independent reviews consistently estimate ~100 US-focused brokers handled rigorously.
- Human review workflow remains a stated differentiator for brokers requiring manual forms, fax, or notarized documentation.
- A November 12 Fox News article on "5 opt-out myths" (the most recent piece in the space) reiterates that opting out of one broker does not remove you from others — implicitly reinforcing the value proposition of subscription services like DeleteMe.

## Incogni
- No new announcements in the last 24–48 hours.
- Portfolio context: Surfshark (Incogni's parent group) acquired a majority stake in Optery on September 7–8, 2026. Incogni and Optery remain separate products with separate teams; no bundling announced yet.
- Coverage: ~420 data broker sites (official figure). An independent audit reportedly passed Incogni's removal claims.
- Pricing on record: Standard $7.99/month, Unlimited $14.99/month (monthly billing). Surfshark-bundled discounts may apply.
- Higher tiers now include an expanded custom removal database and Exposure Scanner feature (expanded June 2026).

## Privacy Bee
- No new announcements in the last 24–48 hours.
- Significant August 2026 development (most recent): PCMag awarded Privacy Bee a perfect 5/5 rating and Editors' Choice — described in press coverage as the first perfect score for a data removal service in PCMag history.
- Also in August 2026: Privacy Bee acquired EraseMe.app, a competing personal data removal service. EraseMe.app customers are being migrated to Privacy Bee's broader removal and monitoring platform.
- Self-reported coverage: 400–1,000+ brokers. The PCMag score may drive increased consumer comparison-shopping against DeleteMe and Incogni.

## Optery
- No new announcements in the last 24–48 hours.
- Key ongoing context: Optery is now majority-owned by Surfshark (deal announced September 7–8, 2026) and operates as a standalone company within the Surfshark Group alongside Incogni and Ironwall.
- OpenAI controversy (ongoing): Optery added AI-powered removal reporting that defaulted users into data sharing with OpenAI. After backlash — including removal from Privacy Guides' recommended services list — Optery made the OpenAI data transfer opt-in and added an internal processing option. Recovery ongoing.
- Pricing: $39/year (Core) to $249/year (Ultimate) per recent third-party comparisons.
- Coverage: 380–625 sites with Expanded Reach enabled (vendor-reported).

## Kanary
- No new announcements in the last 24–48 hours.
- Product note: Kanary has rebranded its primary product as "Kanary Copilot," now primarily a mobile app.
- Compliance: Holds SOC 2 Type 1 attestation (Prescient Security).
- Coverage: ~300 data broker sites.
- Continues to publish competitive content referencing the OneRep reputational controversy to attract customers from that segment.

## Regulatory / Market Context
- **California DROP enforcement is active:** Brokers must access DROP at least every 45 days and complete deletion within 45 days of a request. Only ~30% of registered data brokers were compliant in the first enforcement week. Fines: $200/request/day for non-compliance.
- **Most recent news item (November 12):** A Fox News piece titled "Five data broker opt-out myths that can leave retirees exposed" reinforces the broader public narrative that manual opt-outs are insufficient — consistent with the paid-service industry's core message.
- No legislation, funding announcements, or security incidents affecting any major competitor were found in the last 24–48 hours.

## Market Summary
No significant breaking news emerged in the 24–48 hours ending November 15, 2026. The competitive landscape remains shaped by three macro forces: Surfshark's consolidation of Incogni and Optery into a single privacy group (creating a potential bundling threat to standalone services), Privacy Bee's PCMag perfect-score momentum from August, and the California DROP enforcement regime pushing consumers to ask whether paid services are still worth it alongside a free state platform.
