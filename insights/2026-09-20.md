## Regulatory / Market Context
- **California DROP compliance rate**: Only **9% of data brokers** are currently complying with California's DELETE Act per DROP platform data — enforcement pressure on non-compliant brokers expected to intensify; this is a tailwind for removal services as consumers seek faster results.
- **Connecticut**: Governor signed SB 4 into law (May 27, 2026) adding data broker registration, banning geolocation data sales, and limiting surveillance pricing and genetic data processing — expanding the regulated market.
- **Oregon SB 1587** (effective June 5, 2026): Prohibits state/local government bodies from disclosing PII to data brokers without written attestation that data won't be used for immigration enforcement — narrower scope but notable federal-tension angle.
- **California enforcement fines** (LocateSmarter $116,490; Cybba $52,400) remain the dominant regulatory story; no new enforcement actions in the 24–48 hour window.
- **Surveillance pricing** as an emerging consumer harm narrative: September privacy news cycle is heavily featuring this angle (data brokers feeding algorithmic/AI pricing), potentially increasing general consumer awareness and demand for removal services.

## Market Summary
The September 20 competitive window shows Surfshark Group deepening its bundle advantage — the Dausos protocol and Scam Text Protection additions to Surfshark One plans strengthen the cross-sell environment for both Incogni and the newly acquired Optery.
