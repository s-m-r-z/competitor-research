# Competitor Insights — 2026-08-13

## Privacy Bee
- **Acquisition (Aug 12, 2026):** Privacy Bee acquired EraseMe.app, folding its data-removal technology into their platform. New EraseMe.app visitors are now redirected to Privacy Bee. This expands Privacy Bee's already-broad 1,128 data-broker coverage with new identification and monitoring tooling.
- **PCMag Perfect 5-Star Rating:** Privacy Bee is the first and only data removal service to receive a perfect 5/5 Editors' Choice rating from PCMag (44 years of testing; no service had exceeded 4.5 before). PCMag cited "the most extensive data broker coverage on the market" and coverage of 185,000+ custom sites.
- **Aggressive strategy:** Privacy Bee distinguishes itself by using legal threats, regulatory complaints, and multi-channel pressure on brokers that ignore standard opt-out requests — positioning beyond standard automation.

## Incogni
- **August discounts:** Active 55% off annual plan promotion via coupon codes — likely a customer acquisition push ahead of the California DROP regulation shift.
- **Review surge:** Multiple major outlets (Security.org on Aug 10, CyberInsider on Aug 11) published updated 2026 Incogni reviews, citing its Deloitte-audited coverage of 420+ data-broker websites and 245M+ removal requests processed since January 2022.
- **PCMag rating:** 4.5 stars. Positioned as "top automated choice" in head-to-head comparisons against DeleteMe.

## Optery
- **CNET Best Overall 2026:** Named by CNET as the #1 data removal service of 2026, recognized for the highest third-party-verified removal success rate (65%+) and best transparency (screenshots of where data appears).
- **PCMag Editors' Choice:** Fifth consecutive year of winning this award (2022–2026).
- **2026 Cybersecurity Excellence Awards:** Won in three categories — Attack Surface Management, Anti-Phishing, and Human Risk Management. Also won Globee Award for Social Engineering category.
- **DROP positioning:** Optery published guidance on how it complements California's DROP system by providing continuous monitoring, whereas DROP checks only every 45 days.

## DeleteMe
- **Performance:** Third-party testing rates DeleteMe as a "mid-level performer," with removal rates below Optery and EasyOptOuts. PCMag rating dropped to 3.5 stars (from higher in prior years).
- **Pricing:** Continues at ~$10.75/month (annual plan). No new pricing or feature announcements found in the last 48 hours.
- **DROP impact:** DeleteMe's "we send opt-outs for you" value prop is reduced for California residents who can now use DROP for free; the service will need to sharpen its differentiation around monitoring and non-California brokers.

## OneRep
- **Mozilla partnership ended:** Mozilla has officially cut ties with OneRep (operator of Mozilla Monitor Plus), following the March 2024 Brian Krebs exposé that CEO Dimitri Shelest founded dozens of people-search websites and still operated Nuwber, a data broker.
- **Performance concerns:** OneRep removed only ~42% of listings in third-party testing, placing it in the lower half of the field.
- **SOC 2 Type II certified:** Maintains this certification, providing some assurance on data-security practices despite reputational issues.

## Kanary
- **PCMag rating:** 4.0 stars — solid mid-tier standing.
- **Key differentiator:** Continuous (not scheduled) broker monitoring with faster re-listing alerts than competitors with fixed scan cycles.
- **Coverage:** 300+ data brokers, strong automation, US-focused.

## Removaly
- No significant news or public announcements found in the last 48 hours.

## Market Summary
The biggest story of the week is Privacy Bee's double move — earning PCMag's historic first-ever perfect 5-star rating immediately followed by the acquisition of EraseMe.app on August 12 — signaling an aggressive consolidation play at the premium end of the market. Meanwhile, California's DROP enforcement (effective August 1, 2026) is reshaping competitive positioning across the board: services are pivoting from "we do opt-outs for you" toward continuous monitoring and coverage beyond California-registered brokers, as the free government platform commoditizes basic one-time removal requests for 40M+ California residents.
