# Competitor Insights — 2026-10-27

## DeleteMe
- ISO 27001:2022 certification announced March 2026, positioning DeleteMe as an enterprise-trusted service with independent security audit credentials.
- Current pricing: $129/year (1 person), $229/year (2 people), $329/year (4 people); no monthly billing option available.
- Standard plan covers ~100 US-focused data brokers; higher tiers cover more. Some brokers still require government ID upload.
- A 2024 Consumer Reports study found DeleteMe removed 27% of results within 4 months vs. 70% for manual removal — effectiveness perception risk remains.
- California's DELETE Act (DROP platform) went into mandatory enforcement August 1, 2026; DeleteMe's value proposition weakens for CA residents who can use the state tool for free.
- No new product announcements or pricing changes found in the last 48 hours.

## Privacy Bee
- **Major acquisition:** Privacy Bee acquired EraseMe.app to expand its data removal technology and ongoing privacy monitoring capabilities. Financial terms undisclosed.
- EraseMe.app customers will receive transition instructions; no action required from them until then.
- Earned **PCMag 5/5 Editors' Choice** rating (August 5, 2026) — first and only data removal service to earn a perfect PCMag score; strong differentiation signal.
- Coverage reportedly at 1,128 data brokers at time of PCMag testing (up from ~1,042 earlier in the year).
- No October-specific announcements found, but the EraseMe acquisition is recent and ongoing.

## Incogni
- iOS app launched February 19, 2026; mobile presence expanding.
- Claims to be the first data removal company to publish an independent Deloitte audit — significant trust signal if verified.
- Coverage: 420+ brokers per most comparison sites.
- Pricing: ~$7.49/month billed annually (one of the more affordable options).
- TechRadar rates it among the most efficient and accessible services in 2026.
- Con: No free plan or trial; broker compliance not guaranteed per Incogni's own terms.
- No new October 2026 announcements found.

## Optery
- Ranked **No. 177 on the 2026 Inc. 5000** list in its first-ever appearance, reporting 1,906% three-year revenue growth — strong growth signal.
- CNET named Optery its 2026 "Best Overall Data Removal Service" (per company-published summary).
- Earlier controversy: Optery's ToS was updated to share user data with OpenAI by default; after privacy community backlash, the company reversed this to opt-in only. Community trust partially recovered.
- PCWorld (August 5, 2026) praised the free scan/exposure report but noted real value requires a paid plan.
- No October-specific announcements found.

## Kanary
- Published a **2026 Product Brief** on its blog outlining continuous find/remove/prevent approach with added focus on stalking and doxxing use cases.
- Coverage: 300–350 data brokers (sources vary); strongest on people-search sites, weaker on marketing/B2B data brokers.
- Pricing: Conflicting signals — sources cite $9–$16.99/month depending on plan and billing cycle; a free tier with 3 free removals exists.
- Lacks identity theft insurance; breach/dark web monitoring requires the Advanced plan.
- Reviewers frequently compare it unfavorably to Incogni for long-term automated removals, positioning it more as a "visibility" tool.
- No major October announcements found beyond the product brief.

## OneRep
- No significant new news in 2026.
- Company continues to defend itself against the 2024 KrebsOnSecurity report linking its CEO to people-search networks (Nuwber); Mozilla dropped OneRep as a partner in 2024.
- Current pricing: from $8.33/month (annual billing).
- Coverage: Claims 318 people-search sites and 559 non-public brokers, but no independent verification publicly reported.
- Reputational risk from 2024 controversy continues to appear in 2026 reviews.

## Removaly
- No news or activity found in search results for October 2026. May have low media visibility or limited market presence.

## Market Summary
The most significant development in the last 48 hours is **Privacy Bee's acquisition of EraseMe.app**, expanding its broker coverage and tech stack while its fresh PCMag 5/5 rating sharpens its lead-generation messaging. The broader market continues to be shaped by California's DROP platform (mandatory since August 2026), which is compressing the value proposition for all services among California consumers and will likely force differentiation toward national/enterprise coverage, speed, and certification credibility.
