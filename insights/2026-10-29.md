# Competitor Insights — 2026-10-29

## DeleteMe
- International enrollment has ended as of September 21, 2026; the service is now explicitly positioned as US-only.
- Markets itself as covering 750+ data brokers, but third-party reviews (CyberInsider, October 2026) note that roughly 100 are handled automatically, with 800+ requiring custom requests — a gap that reviewers flag as a marketing-vs-reality concern.
- California's DROP platform (free, state-run, 600+ brokers) is an emerging pressure on DeleteMe's value proposition for California residents, who can now submit a single deletion request at no cost.

## Privacy Bee
- Earned PCMag's first-ever perfect 5-star / Editors' Choice score for a data removal service (August 5–6, 2026), which the company is actively promoting across press channels.
- Acquired EraseMe.app (August 12, 2026), migrating its customers onto Privacy Bee's platform and incorporating EraseMe.app's technology to strengthen the core service.
- Claims coverage of 1,128 data brokers and 185,000+ custom sites — the highest broker count advertised among major competitors.
- No significant news in the last 48 hours; company appears in a holding/promotion phase after August announcements.

## Incogni
- Running a Cybersecurity Awareness Week promotion (around October 24, 2026): 58% off automated data removal plans.
- October 2026 AllAboutCookies review reports an 89% completion rate (850 of 959 requests) in independent testing — and notes that Deloitte has verified Incogni's removal claims, positioning this as a credibility differentiator.
- Custom Removals feature expanded to 3,000+ unique domains in early 2026 and is now included in Unlimited, Family Unlimited, and Protect plans.
- Priced from $7.99/month; no free tier or trial remains a noted weakness in reviews.
- BBB accreditation earned in 2025 is still highlighted as a trust signal.

## Optery
- Won Cyber Defense Magazine's 2026 "Transformational Anti-Phishing Award" (announced October 26, 2026) for its workforce data removal offering — security/IT teams can enroll employees via a self-service dashboard to reduce personal-data exposure used in phishing attacks.
- October 21 blog post covers data brokers used to target law firms, reinforcing Optery's positioning in the enterprise/professional segment.
- October 15 CyberInsider review notes Optery recently introduced optional AI-powered removals running on OpenAI.
- PCMag rates Optery at 4.5 stars (vs. Privacy Bee's 5.0 and Incogni's 4.5).

## OneRep
- Optery's website (last modified October 14, 2026) continues to highlight the earlier Krebs on Security investigation into OneRep's ties to data broker operations — this remains an active reputational risk for OneRep. No direct response from OneRep was found in this cycle's results.
- Appears in roundup comparisons but no new product or pricing announcements detected.

## Kanary
- No breaking news in the last 48 hours.
- Recent coverage notes a product pivot: Kanary now operates primarily as a mobile app called "Kanary Copilot" (new users can only access Copilot, per a January 2026 OneRep review).
- Pricing remains inconsistently reported: one source cites $9.99/month (Pro) and $50/month (Advanced); Kanary's own 2026 product brief (updated July) references $25/$50 monthly tiers; annualized plans at ~$250/$500 are also cited. Verify at kanary.com/pricing.
- Coverage of 300+ data brokers; reviewers note it is expensive relative to competitors.

## Emerging / Other Services
- **EasyOptOuts** appears in privacy-community comparisons at $19.99/year — gaining mentions as a budget alternative to subscription services.
- **California DROP platform**: The state's free deletion platform (600+ registered brokers, August 1, 2026 enforcement start) is creating a market-level headwind for lower-tier paid services targeting California residents.
- Data brokers were accused of hiding opt-out pages from Google (Fox News/Senate probe, March 14, 2026 report) — this background narrative continues to give removal services a tailwind.

## Market Summary
Cybersecurity Awareness Week (October 2026) is driving promotional activity industry-wide, with Incogni's 58%-off offer being the most visible near-term tactic; Privacy Bee holds the strongest earned-media position after its PCMag 5-star win and EraseMe.app acquisition, while Optery is differentiating toward enterprise anti-phishing use cases and California's free DROP platform continues to pressure the consumer-tier value proposition across all paid services.
