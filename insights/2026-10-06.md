## Regulatory & Market Context
- **Connecticut's sweeping new privacy law took effect October 1, 2026** — directly relevant to all players. The law adds a data broker regulatory framework, bans sale of precise geolocation data, restricts use of consumer data for personalized algorithmic pricing, prohibits genetic data sales, and gives residents the right to request deletion even of publicly sourced records
- **Legal challenge filed September 17, 2026**: Spokeo, PeopleConnect, and other people-search companies sued Connecticut AG William Tong seeking to block enforcement, citing First Amendment grounds — outcome will set precedent for similar state laws
- Similar restrictions recently enacted in Oregon and Virginia; Maryland and New Jersey went further, banning all sensitive information sales
- **California's DELETE Act** (DROP platform) now operational in 2026: allows CA residents to delete data from 543+ registered brokers via a single government portal — reduces the urgency of paid opt-out services for California residents specifically
- Market dynamic: growing regulatory pressure is a tailwind for services but also creates a floor of free/government options that could commoditize the space
