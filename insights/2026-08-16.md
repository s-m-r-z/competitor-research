# Competitor Insights — 2026-08-16

## Optery
- **Summer Sale promotion active:** Optery is running a Summer 2026 promotion — promo code `SUMMER2026` unlocks 20% off all paid plans. Pricing tiers without the discount: Core ($3.99/mo), Extended ($14.99/mo), Ultimate ($24.99/mo / ~$249/yr). This is the first promotional pricing action detected from Optery in the current tracking period.
- Continues to be ranked #1 overall data removal service by CNET and PCMag (4.5 stars), with screenshot-based proof of removals remaining its primary differentiator.

## Incogni
- **55% off August promotion:** Incogni is running an aggressive August discount (code `IncogniCNL`) bringing annual plans to roughly $40–45/year — one of the lowest price points among full-coverage services. Multiple coupon aggregators are surfacing this deal, suggesting a paid acquisition push.
- **Named clear winner vs. DeleteMe in 2026 head-to-head tests:** CyberInsider published updated test results showing Incogni at nearly 3x the first-week removal rate vs. DeleteMe. Cited strengths: speed, automation cadence (every 60–90 days), and the Deloitte third-party audit (August 2025) verifying 420+ broker coverage and 245M+ removal requests processed.
- Two major review refreshes published this week: CyberInsider (Aug 10) and AllAboutCookies (Aug 12).

## DeleteMe
- No new product announcements or pricing changes detected.
- Confirmed current pricing: $129/yr (1 person), $229/yr (2 people), $329/yr (4-person family).
- PCMag rating (3.5 stars) is now the lowest among the top-five services — trailing Privacy Bee (5.0), Optery (4.5), Incogni (4.5), and Kanary (4.0). The brand's 15-year track record and reputation remain key retention arguments, but new customer acquisition is likely under pressure.

## EasyOptOuts
- **Emerging budget disruptor gaining media attention:** At $19.99/year, EasyOptOuts is drawing renewed coverage as the cheapest automated option in the market. Consumer Reports' 2024 study showed a 65% removal rate — on par with Optery and ahead of DeleteMe. Multiple 2026 review sites are recommending it for cost-conscious users willing to accept partial coverage.
- Limitation: No live dashboard; users receive only email summaries. Covers 200+ brokers on 3 automated scans/year.
- **Competitive risk:** At ~$20/year, EasyOptOuts undercuts Optery Core ($39/yr), Incogni (~$78/yr), and DeleteMe ($129/yr) dramatically. Its presence is likely suppressing willingness to pay among price-sensitive segments.

## RemoveMe (iolo)
- **New entrant:** RemoveMe, built by iolo (the PC software company behind System Mechanic), launched in late 2025 and is gaining review coverage in 2026. Offers flexible monthly billing at $9.99/month (billed quarterly at $29.99) — an unusual pricing model in a market dominated by annual subscriptions.
- Positions itself as a direct response to the consumer privacy crisis. Niche: users who want monthly flexibility rather than committing to an annual plan.

## OneRep
- Mozilla Monitor Plus (powered by OneRep) officially shut down December 17, 2025. OneRep is now operating as a standalone service without Mozilla's distribution channel.
- Current offering: 310+ public data broker sites, SOC 2 Type II certified, 5-day free trial + 30-day money-back guarantee. Pricing ranges $8.33–$55.95/month depending on plan and family size.
- A new "Pro" tier was introduced, expanding coverage to non-public data brokers with unlimited custom removal requests and a dedicated privacy expert — a feature set targeting users who previously relied on the Mozilla relationship.
- Blog strategy: OneRep published a Privacy Bee review (competitive awareness signal), and Incogni published a OneRep review — the top competitors are actively targeting each other's search traffic.

## Privacy Bee
- No new announcements today (major news — PCMag 5-star rating and EraseMe.app acquisition — covered in yesterday's 08-15 insights).
- Continues to dominate with 1,128 broker coverage and broadest market positioning.

## Kanary
- No product announcements or pricing changes detected today.
- Continues to be highlighted as the top choice for users seeking 95–100% removal rates via its hybrid automated/manual approach and monthly rescans. Holds PCMag 4.0-star rating.

## Regulatory: California DROP — Post-August 1 Market Adjustment
- Enforcement of California's DROP platform is now active. Law firms (Alston & Bird, Clark Hill, Benesch) published compliance guides this week for data brokers, indicating the industry is actively responding to the $200/day/request fine structure.
- Over 300,000+ Californians have enrolled in DROP, with 500+ registered brokers now legally required to process those requests every 45 days.
- **Industry implication confirmed:** The operational reality of DROP is beginning to reshape competitive messaging. Services will need to articulate value beyond California geography — e.g., national coverage, non-people-search brokers, and speed of removal beyond DROP's 90-day processing window.

## Market Summary
Today's competitive landscape is defined by promotional pricing wars at the premium end (Incogni's 55% August discount, Optery's SUMMER2026 sale) and growing pressure from budget disruptor EasyOptOuts at $19.99/year — while the California DROP enforcement deadline's aftermath is forcing the entire market to re-articulate its value proposition beyond what a free government tool now provides for California residents.
