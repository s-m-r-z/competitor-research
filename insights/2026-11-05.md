# Competitor Insights — 2026-11-05

## DeleteMe
- No major product announcements detected in the last 24–48 hours.
- Earlier 2026 milestone: acquired Block Party (social media security tool) in March 2026, expanding beyond traditional data broker removal into social-layer privacy protection.
- Security.org's 2026 roundup labels DeleteMe "Most Thorough" but notes Incogni found more records (129) in a one-week head-to-head test.
- Trustpilot rating sits at 3.9/5 as of July 2026, described as "mixed" customer sentiment.
- Consumer Reports 2024 field study measured DeleteMe at ~27% removal rate after four months — lowest among paid services tested; this figure continues to surface in competitor marketing.
- Pricing steady at ~$129/year per individual plan.

## Privacy Bee
- No new announcements in last 48 hours; most recent significant activity was August 2026.
- Earned a perfect 5.0 "Exemplary" PCMag Editors' Choice rating in August 2026 — the first perfect score PCMag has awarded a data removal service; PCMag placed Optery and Incogni at 4.5 for context.
- Acquired EraseMe.app on August 12, 2026, redirecting EraseMe visitors to Privacy Bee and absorbing its technology into the platform.
- Named All About Cookies' best service for digital footprint removal in February 2026.
- Now covers 1,128 data brokers — the largest stated broker coverage footprint in the category.
- No independent critical coverage found; most positive coverage is company-sourced or from affiliate partners.

## Incogni
- No fresh announcements in last 48 hours; still in seasonal promotion window (Black Friday discounts were active as of yesterday's report).
- Deloitte limited assurance report (July 2025) confirmed 245+ million removal requests completed; data not sold to third parties.
- Covers 420+ brokers with re-request cycles every 60 days (public brokers) or 90 days (private brokers).
- Pricing: $95.88/year annual plan, ~$15.98/month monthly.
- Remains under Nord Security / Surfshark ownership umbrella.

## Optery
- No new announcements in last 48 hours.
- Recently added Map Blurring control for Extended and Ultimate subscribers; expanded service availability to Canada (excluding Quebec).
- Mobile apps now available on Android and iOS.
- SOC 2 Type II certification maintained; received 2026 Fortress Cybersecurity Award in privacy enhancing technologies.
- 2024 OpenAI data-sharing controversy (reversed to opt-in) continues to surface in trust discussions; no new incidents noted.
- Plan tiers: Core (~90 sites), Extended (~240), Ultimate (~370+); independently ranked highest for removal rate by Consumer Reports (68%).

## Kanary
- No breaking news in last 48 hours.
- CNET's July 2026 review highlighted Kanary as the only tested service covering data brokers, search engines, risky sites, social media, AND LLMs simultaneously — a potential differentiator as AI-aggregated data exposure becomes a concern.
- Achieved SOC 2 Type 1 certification (Prescient Security audit) on March 17, 2026.
- Pricing: Core at $250/year, Advanced at $500/year — highest among mainstream competitors.
- TechRadar notes removals take slightly longer than competitors; independent PrivacyOn testing reported only ~6 profiles removed in the first two weeks.
- Covers 300+ data brokers and people-search sites; strong on US-based threats like doxxing and harassment scenarios.

## Emerging Players / Market
- **CyberForget** continues to appear in 2026 comparison roundups alongside DeleteMe, Incogni, and Optery; positions on full opt-out lifecycle automation (scan → submit → verify → monitor → resubmit).
- **FTC warning (November 4, 2026)**: An FTC-attributed report circulating notes that opting out one person's profile can still leave family members exposed through shared address and public record links — a potential angle for removal services to market "household" or "family" plans.

## Regulatory / Market Context
- **California DROP milestone**: The platform has surpassed 590,000 enrollees with 680+ registered brokers as of November 2026; all brokers are now obligated to have completed their initial download/upload cycle this month per the state's compliance timeline.
- Brokers must access DROP at least every 45 days; non-compliance carries $200/day fines per outstanding request — ongoing cost pressure on smaller brokers.
- DROP covers California residents only, keeping the paid removal service market intact in other 49 states.

## Market Summary
The competitive landscape this week shows no dramatic 24–48 hour shifts, but Privacy Bee's August momentum (perfect PCMag rating, EraseMe acquisition, 1,128 broker coverage) has repositioned it as a credible challenger to the DeleteMe/Incogni/Optery tier. OneRep continues its reputational decline post-Mozilla, and California's DROP compliance deadline this month may accelerate broker consolidation, indirectly shrinking the addressable market for removal services over the medium term.
