# Competitor Insights — 2026-09-07

> **Note:** This report covers the September 7 slot; due to infrastructure restarts it was filed on September 8. No major breaking announcements detected in the 24–48 hour window of September 5–7. Updates below reflect the freshest available intelligence.

## DeleteMe
- **No new announcements in the September 5–7 window.** The service remains active with 750+ broker coverage and quarterly manual removal reports.
- Block Party acquisition (March 24, 2026) continues to be integrated; no new product shipping milestones announced yet.
- TD SYNNEX channel partnership (April 2026) and ISO 27001:2022 certification remain the primary enterprise sales differentiators.
- Pricing stable: $129/year (individual) to $499/2-year (family).

## Privacy Bee
- **No new announcements this window.** The August news cycle (PCMag perfect 5.0-star rating, EraseMe.app acquisition) continues to drive inbound attention.
- Coverage claim now at 1,128 data brokers — far ahead of Incogni (420+) and Kanary (300+).
- EraseMe.app customer migration to Privacy Bee infrastructure is ongoing; watch for any integration-related reviews or complaints surfacing on Reddit/Trustpilot.

## Incogni
- **No new product announcements.** Stable competitor with Deloitte-audited removal process as main credibility signal.
- Coverage: 420+ brokers (standard), up to 2,420 with Unlimited plan.
- Ongoing PCMag gap vs. Privacy Bee (4.5 vs. 5.0 stars) is a marketing liability that Surfshark/Incogni has not publicly addressed.
- Pricing: $7.99/month; bundled into Surfshark One+.

## OneRep
- **No new announcements this window.**
- 550+ non-public broker coverage (expanded May 2026) and data breach monitoring alerts remain the service's headline features.
- Mozilla Monitor Plus partnership termination (founder conflict of interest) continues to be a reputational liability in enterprise/B2B conversations; no remediation actions announced.

## Kanary
- **Kanary Copilot mobile app:** Kanary has transitioned its primary interface to a mobile app (Kanary Copilot), while retaining web access for existing subscribers. This is a significant UX shift that may affect enterprise and power-user retention.
- **X (Twitter) integration (June 2026):** Advanced members now get automated scans for doxxing, personal data leaks, and impersonation on X — actionable removal steps included.
- **LinkedIn coverage (July 2026):** Advanced tier now covers LinkedIn directly, adding the most important professional network to its scope. Noteworthy competitive move as LinkedIn is a major personal data exposure surface.
- **CNET review (July 2026):** Coverage arguing bulk data broker removal alone is insufficient for modern risk — positions Kanary's hybrid monitoring approach favorably.
- Pricing: $14.99–$16.99/month (annually). Coverage: 300+ brokers.

## Removaly
- **Status: Defunct.** Ceased operations 2022; technology now powers DuckDuckGo Privacy Pro's Personal Information Removal feature.

## DuckDuckGo Privacy Pro
- No new announcements. Bundle ($9.99/month or $99.99/year) covering VPN, identity theft restoration, and ~50+ broker removals remains a mainstream-friendly but coverage-limited option.

## EasyOptOuts (Emerging Budget Competitor)
- **Price point to watch:** At $19.99/year, EasyOptOuts is an order of magnitude cheaper than most dedicated services and covers ~160 brokers (primarily people-search sites). Independent testing has rated it effective vs. services costing 5–10x more.
- Fully automated, minimal data collection model. Rescans and resubmits every 4 months (3x/year).
- Not widely reviewed yet — positioned to capture price-sensitive consumers who might otherwise choose paid services or DIY opt-outs. Should be tracked as a potential market-share risk at the low end.

## ReputationDefender / MyPrivacy (AOL)
- Premium end of the market ($1,000–$10,000/year). Covers ~100 people-search sites plus reputation management, dark web monitoring, and Google search result management.
- Not a direct competitor for mass-market customers but relevant for high-net-worth and executive protection segments.

## Regulatory & Market Context
- **California DROP enforcement phase (active since August 1, 2026):** 500+ registered California brokers must now check DROP every 45 days and process deletions or face $200/day penalties. No major enforcement actions publicly announced yet — the September–November window will reveal compliance rates.
- **FTC PADFAA enforcement (February 2026):** Warning letters to 13 data brokers for potential violations involving military personnel data remain the most recent federal enforcement action. Kochava settlement (May 2026) restricting sensitive location data sales is the most recent concluded action.
- **Multi-state privacy enforcement consortium:** Formed in late 2025, coordinating simultaneous investigations across states. Expected to increase enforcement velocity in Q3–Q4 2026.
- **Connecticut:** Data broker registration takes effect October 1, 2026 — imminent. Sale prohibition begins January 1, 2027.

## Market Summary
No major product launches or competitive moves occurred in the September 5–7 window; the industry is in a post-summer-announcement consolidation period. The most strategically notable trend to watch is Kanary's mobile-first pivot (Copilot app) and LinkedIn/X platform coverage expansions, which differentiate it from purely people-search-site focused rivals, while EasyOptOuts's low price point ($19.99/year) represents an underpriced emerging threat at the budget end of the market.
