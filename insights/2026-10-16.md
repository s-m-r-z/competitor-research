# Competitor Insights — 2026-10-16

## DeleteMe
- Pricing remains $129/year (1 person), $229/year (2), $329/year (4); no monthly plan available
- Claims 141M+ successful opt-out removals completed to date (self-reported figure)
- Standard plan covers ~100 US-focused brokers; higher tiers claim up to 750+
- Some brokers still require an optional government ID upload to complete removal
- Data re-appears within 3–6 months after cancellation; no prorated refunds policy noted by reviewers
- No major product announcements or press releases detected in the last 24–48 hours

## Privacy Bee
- **Acquisition:** Acquired EraseMe.app (announced ~August 15, 2026) to expand data removal technology and ongoing privacy monitoring; financial terms undisclosed; existing subscribers unaffected during integration
- **PCMag rating:** Awarded a perfect 5/5 stars and Editors' Choice (August 5, 2026) — reported as PCMag's highest-rated data removal service
- ZDNET called it "one of the most comprehensive data removal services I've tested" in a head-to-head with DeleteMe
- TechRadar rated it 4.5/5; CyberInsider notes it is more expensive than some competitors
- No new announcements detected in the last 24–48 hours beyond the August acquisition coverage

## Kanary
- **2026 Product Brief published:** Free Community tier, Professional tier at $9.99/month (no annual commitment required), Advanced at ~$500/year; Enterprise pricing varies by threat model
- Coverage of 300+ broker sites via a hybrid automated + manual approach
- Initial full removal results take 30–90 days per TechRadar review
- Security.org testing found relatively slow initial results (6 profiles removed in 2 weeks)
- Described as VC-backed in competitor comparisons, with associated risk of pricing/pivot changes
- No new funding or product announcements detected in the last 24–48 hours

## Removaly
- **Effectively defunct:** Multiple sources confirm Removaly ceased operations or went inactive around early 2022; removaly.com domain confirmed non-resolving as of July 5, 2026
- Should be removed from competitive tracking as an active player

## OneRep
- Ongoing reputational issue: CEO Dimitri Shelest linked to a dozen+ people-search data broker sites (originally reported by KrebsOnSecurity)
- Mozilla ended its partnership; one source dates the official end to December 17, 2025
- Unverified reports of 2026 product additions: European and Canadian broker coverage, risk-scoring dashboard
- No new confirmed announcements in the last 24–48 hours

## Incogni (Surfshark)
- Current pricing ~$7.99/month on annual billing
- Coverage claims: 420+ brokers (Standard plan) to 2,000+ (Unlimited); independent sources dispute the higher figures, citing ~180 core automated brokers
- Deloitte-verified automated removals mentioned in marketing materials
- No new product announcements detected in the last 24–48 hours

## Optery
- Self-claims CNET "Editors' Choice" for personal data removal in 2026
- Consumer Reports 2024 testing found Optery removed 68% of test profiles in four months — top performer among paid services
- No new product announcements detected in the last 24–48 hours

## Emerging / Notable
- **RemoveMe (RealDefense):** Launched October 2025; PCWorld October 2026 sponsored coverage cites 400+ data broker sites covered with automated removal and monitoring
- **BlackCloak DBR Engine:** Redesigned in May 2026; uses AI to understand per-site opt-out workflows; targets enterprise/executive protection market
- **California DROP Platform:** State-run free deletion tool covering 543+ registered brokers; August 1, 2026 was the deadline for brokers to begin processing deletion requests (90-day window); competes indirectly with paid services for California residents

## Regulatory Landscape
- Senate JEC report (February 2026) named Comscore, IQVIA Digital, Telesign, and 6sense for hiding opt-out pages from Google; a May 2026 supplement confirms Findem made its opt-out page searchable following the report
- No federal privacy law passed; oversight remains patchwork by state

## Market Summary
The data broker opt-out market saw no major competitor announcements in the past 24–48 hours; the most significant recent development is Privacy Bee's EraseMe.app acquisition (August 2026), which strengthens its technology moat and positions it as the highest-reviewed service per PCMag. California's DROP platform continues to create indirect pricing pressure on paid services for the state's 40M residents, while Removaly's confirmed shutdown reduces the competitive field.
