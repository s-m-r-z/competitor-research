# Competitor Insights — 2026-07-26

## DeleteMe
- Remains the most established service at $129/year per person; family plans available
- Manual, risk-prioritized opt-out approach covering 122 sites with 90-day re-check cycles
- US-headquartered with strongest coverage of US-specific people-search sites alongside Optery
- No new product announcements in the last 48 hours; positioned as the incumbent/safe choice in review roundups

## Incogni
- Running a 55% discount promotion verified for July 2026 (coupon code IncogniCNL on annual plans)
- Family Unlimited plan priced at $275.88/year; 30-day money-back guarantee on all plans
- Comparison reviews updated July 16, 2026 with new pricing, features, and broker-coverage numbers
- Credibility highlight: third-party Deloitte audit verifying removal practices continues to be a key differentiator
- Positive mid-July Trustpilot reviews citing reduced spam calls/emails; AES-256 encryption noted

## Optery
- Won the **Market Disruptor** award in the Digital Risk Protection category at the 14th Annual Global InfoSec Awards (RSAC 2026, March 2026) — significant industry recognition
- Covers 630+ data broker sites, the broadest coverage among reviewed services
- Patented search technology combined with opt-out automation; "live screenshots" provide proof of deletions
- Maintains a genuinely useful free tier (scan before committing) with flexible paid plans
- Consistently rated best overall data removal service across TechRadar, Security.org, and cyberinsider.com roundups

## Privacy Bee
- Priced at $18/month base; strong "set-and-forget" positioning covering 400+ sites including directories
- Differentiators: family coverage, tracker blocking, and breach alerts bundled into the service
- Continues to be recommended for users wanting more than basic broker opt-outs and willing to pay a premium

## OneRep
- Fully automated removal workflow: submit info → system scans broker database → opt-outs submitted automatically
- Featured as a top Incogni alternative in competitor blog posts (onerep.com publishing comparison content)
- No major product announcements in the last 48 hours

## Kanary
- **Major product pivot**: Kanary has relaunched as **Kanary Copilot**, a mobile-first iOS app (Android waitlist open)
- Gamification strategy: privacy tasks are presented as completable cards during downtime (~10 min each)
- Pricing: free basic tier; premium at $9.99/month for full functionality — lowest monthly price in the segment
- Covers 300+ brokers with automated scans; also handles Google/Facebook/social site removals
- Progress dashboards show proof of deletions
- Important limitation: US-only service; no support for users outside the US

## Removaly
- **Confirmed shutdown**: Removaly was acquired by DuckDuckGo (November 2022) and ceased operations as a standalone service
- DuckDuckGo integrated the technology and expertise into its own privacy offerings
- Multiple "Removaly alternatives" guides are now live, redirecting former customers to Incogni, Optery, and others
- Opportunity: former Removaly users are an acquisition target for competing services

## Emerging / Industry Trends
- **California DROP Platform** (Delete Request and Opt-Out Platform) launched January 2026 under the Delete Act (SB 362); lets any CA resident send a single deletion request to all 600+ registered data brokers simultaneously, for free. Starting **August 1, 2026**, brokers are legally required to process requests or face $200/request/day fines — a major regulatory pressure point affecting all players
- **California Data Broker Strike Force** launched by the CA Privacy Protection Agency to enforce the Delete Act
- **US Senate investigation** found that several major data brokers (Comscore, IQVIA Digital, Telesign, 6sense Insights) placed code on opt-out pages to prevent search engine indexing — ongoing regulatory scrutiny
- The data broker industry is estimated at $200B+ annually with 4,000+ active brokers; data reappears within 3–6 months after removal, sustaining recurring subscription demand

## Market Summary
The competitive landscape is consolidating around a few well-funded players (Incogni, Optery, DeleteMe) while Kanary makes a bold mobile-first pivot and Removaly's exit creates a window to capture displaced subscribers. The looming August 1 California DROP enforcement deadline is the dominant macro story — it both legitimizes consumer demand for these services and introduces a free government alternative that could pressure lower-end pricing tiers.
