- **Independent Deloitte audit**: Incogni continues to cite a Deloitte third-party audit verifying its removal practices — a credibility differentiator none of the other major services currently match at this level.
- **Broker coverage**: Now covers 420+ brokers (automated), with broker network expansion noted in 2026 reviews.
- Strategy continues to converge with Nord Security's identity protection suite (NordProtect "Protect" plan, noted yesterday) — effectively turning Incogni into a module within a broader security product.

## Regulatory: California DROP — 10 Days Into Enforcement
- As of August 11, DROP enforcement has been live for 10 days (effective August 1, 2026).
- **300,000+ consumer deletion requests** were queued at the platform's January 2026 launch; brokers must now process them within 90 days or face **$200/day per unprocessed request** fines.
- Access cadence: Brokers must check DROP every 45 days minimum — the next mandatory check for the first wave falls in mid-September 2026.
- Hashing standardization (8-digit date strings, 10-digit phone numbers without dashes) closes the data format mismatch loophole that historically let brokers evade compliance.
- **Impact on market**: California's 39M residents can now opt out en masse for free, which could suppress demand for paid removal services among CA consumers. Services are responding by emphasizing non-CA broker coverage, speed advantages, and ongoing proactive monitoring.
- 19 states now have comprehensive privacy laws (16 in effect); a federal framework remains absent but regulatory momentum is accelerating.

## Market Summary
The competitive story today centers on Kanary publishing a formal 2026 product brief anchored by new account-settings automation (Google/Twitch), signaling a strategic expansion from pure data removal toward broader online safety hygiene. Meanwhile, California's DROP enforcement system is now 10 days in, with $200/day fines actively accruing for non-compliant brokers — a structural shift that raises the regulatory floor for the entire industry while simultaneously pressuring the value proposition of paid removal services for California's 39M consumers.
