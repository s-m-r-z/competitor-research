# Competitor Insights — 2026-10-20

## DeleteMe
- Published content on California's DROP platform (Oct. 9, 2026), positioning DeleteMe as a complement to the free state tool for residents wanting broader, automated coverage.
- Launched a "Going Ghost" live workshop series tied to Cybersecurity Awareness Month; paired with an AI-in-social-engineering report co-produced with 451 Research.
- ISO 27001:2022 certification (announced March 2026) continues to be cited in reviews as a trust differentiator.
- Block Party (social media privacy tool) acquisition from earlier in the year is still being integrated; features not yet folded into the main product.

## Incogni
- No new product announcements or pricing changes found for October 2026.
- Custom Removals feature (covering 3,000+ sites beyond the 420+ automated brokers) is now highlighted in support documentation as exclusive to Unlimited, Family Unlimited, and Incogni Protect tiers.
- Deloitte ISAE 3000 assurance report from August 2025 continues to be cited as a credibility signal in third-party reviews.
- Starting price holds at $7.99/month; no free tier.

## OneRep
- No new October activity found. Company remains in a weakened position following Mozilla's termination of the Monitor Plus partnership over CEO Dimitri Shelest's ties to people-search businesses.
- Mozilla Monitor Plus has shut down and associated removal data was deleted; former customers must re-engage brokers directly.
- Coverage estimated at ~235 people-search sites in recent reviews — lower than most major competitors.

## Optery
- Released an open-source data broker directory on GitHub covering 640+ brokers, complete with opt-out links and removal guides — a notable community/brand-building move.
- Coverage claims vary widely across sources (100+ to 1,000+ brokers), suggesting ongoing inconsistency in how the company positions its tiers.
- No new pricing changes or product announcements found for October 2026.

## Privacy Bee
- Acquired EraseMe.app in August 2026 to expand automated removal technology; existing subscribers unaffected, EraseMe customers awaiting transition instructions.
- Became the first data removal service to receive a perfect 5/5 PCMag Editors' Choice rating (August 5, 2026); claims coverage of 1,128 data brokers with automated removals on 1,100+.
- No new October-specific announcements found; the PCMag rating and EraseMe acquisition are the leading recent competitive developments.

## Kanary
- No official product announcements or pricing changes found for October 2026.
- Third-party reviews continue to flag slow removal speeds (six profiles removed after two weeks in one test) as a key weakness vs. peers.
- "Kanary Copilot" app targeting streamers (Twitch chat monitoring, doxxing prevention) represents a niche vertical expansion not replicated by competitors.
- Pricing conflict across reviews: $9.99/month (Security.org) vs. $16.99/month rolling or $14.99/month annual (TechRadar) — prospective customers should verify directly.

## Removaly
- No significant news, product updates, or press coverage found for October 2026. Service does not appear in recent best-of rankings with the same frequency as Incogni, DeleteMe, or Privacy Bee.

## Market Summary
California's DROP platform — now in active enforcement with 475,000+ opt-out requests filed and the first enforcement action taken against LocateSmarter — is reshaping the competitive landscape by offering a free state-run alternative, pressuring paid services to differentiate on speed, breadth (non-California brokers), and AI-assisted monitoring. Privacy Bee's PCMag sweep and acquisition signal aggressive consolidation at the top, while OneRep's ongoing fallout from the Mozilla break and Removaly's low visibility suggest continued consolidation among the leading 4–5 players.
