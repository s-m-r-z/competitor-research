# Competitor Insights — 2026-09-16

## Optery (Surfshark Group)
- Nine days after the September 7, 2026 acquisition announcement, no integration changes or service disruptions have been reported; Optery continues operating independently with its own team and technology.
- Surfshark framed the acquisition as a response to growing demand for data control in an AI-driven world, where personal information increasingly surfaces in search results and AI-generated summaries — expect this messaging to intensify in marketing.
- Optery's screenshot-based reporting (visual evidence of where data was found) continues to be cited as a key differentiator vs. competitors like Incogni and DeleteMe that offer less granular transparency.
- Coverage: 1,360+ data broker sites; pricing: Free Basic / Core $39/year / Extended $149/year / Ultimate $249/year.

## Incogni (Surfshark Group)
- Third-party comparison site CyberInsider updated an Aura vs. Incogni guide on **September 15, 2026** (yesterday), reinforcing Incogni's position as "the most hands-off personal data removal service on the market" — fresh SEO-driven visibility boost going into the fall season.
- September promotional campaign (55% off annual plans, code `IncogniCNL`) remains active and is being prominently featured on coupon aggregator sites.
- GenAI & LLM Privacy Ranking 2026 blog post continues to attract press coverage, highlighting Microsoft Copilot, Meta AI, and Kimi as worst performers — a content marketing play that positions Incogni ahead of the AI privacy concern wave.
- Surfshark Group now controls Incogni + Optery + Ironwall, the deepest portfolio in the sector.

## Privacy Bee
- **EraseMe.app acquisition confirmed**: Privacy Bee acquired EraseMe.app, a personal data removal startup with novel tooling for consumer exposure reduction. The technology will be incorporated into Privacy Bee's platform to strengthen existing subscribers' coverage — no pricing or timeline change announced.
- Coverage has grown to **1,128 data brokers** and **185,000+ supported custom sites**, up from earlier 2026 figures, cementing its lead as the broadest-coverage service in the market.
- PCMag **perfect 5-star / Editors' Choice** award (August 6, 2026) is still the defining PR win of the quarter; no other data removal service has achieved a perfect PCMag rating.
- Weekly dashboard refresh cadence (vs. monthly for most competitors) continues to be a meaningful differentiator for high-risk users.

## DeleteMe
- No new product announcements or pricing changes in the last 48 hours.
- Remains on a hybrid model: 950+ sites covered, ~85 automated, remainder manual with 90-day re-check cycles.
- Pricing: $129/year (individual), $499/2 years (family). The manual-heavy approach continues to be questioned in comparative reviews against more automated competitors.
- California DROP Act (full enforcement since August 1, 2026) sustains consumer awareness and inbound demand but also provides a free alternative for California residents that may suppress conversions for lower-tier plans.

## OneRep
- No new product announcements in the last 48 hours.
- The **May 2026 Pro tier launch** — adding coverage of 550+ non-public data brokers, custom removal requests, and data breach monitoring — continues to be the most cited differentiation point in third-party reviews.
- Ongoing reputational risk: founder's ownership stake in Nuwber (a covered data broker) is still being flagged in competitor-authored reviews and consumer advisory content, most recently in an Incogni blog post.
- Content marketing activity: OneRep's Kanary review post (onerep.com/blog/kanary-review) continues to rank in search, reinforcing competitive positioning in the mid-market.

## Kanary
- No product announcements, pricing changes, or notable press activity in the last 48 hours.
- Third-party testing continues to flag slow removal pace (~6 profiles in 2 weeks; ~19% of removals within 32 hours) as the primary weakness vs. Incogni and Optery.
- Strengths remain: handles difficult brokers (LexisNexis, TruthFinder, Radaris) that many competitors skip, and offers transparent reporting on which brokers were contacted and when.
- Coverage: 300+ sites; pricing: $9.99/month.

## Removaly
- No news, announcements, or third-party coverage found in the 48-hour window.

## Regulatory / Market Context
- California DROP enforcement continues (since August 1, 2026): registered brokers must process deletion requests every 45 days; $200/day fine per unfulfilled request. Market awareness is still climbing.
- The Surfshark/Optery acquisition (the second major acquisition in the sector after Surfshark's earlier Incogni rollup) is prompting analyst commentary about further consolidation — independents like DeleteMe, Privacy Bee, and Kanary may face M&A interest.
- Privacy Bee's EraseMe.app acquisition signals that even established players are pursuing inorganic growth to expand coverage capabilities, not just organic broker additions.

## Market Summary
The dominant stories remain **Surfshark Group's accelerating consolidation** (now owning Incogni + Optery + Ironwall) and **Privacy Bee's widening coverage lead** (1,128 brokers, perfect PCMag score, EraseMe.app acquisition), while independent players DeleteMe, OneRep, and Kanary show no major new moves — creating a widening gap between consolidating acquirers and organic-only competitors.
