# Competitor Insights — 2026-10-30

## DeleteMe
- Broker coverage claims vary: DeleteMe's own marketing cites 750+ data brokers, while a third-party count in September 2026 logged 969 listed brokers.
- International enrollment remains closed as of September 21, 2026; the service is now US-only for new sign-ups.
- Independent testing (Consumer Reports) placed DeleteMe sixth of eight services tested, with only 27% of participant data removed — Optery and EasyOptOuts scored significantly higher.
- California's DROP platform (launched January 1, 2026, mandatory processing from August 1, 2026) creates a free alternative for CA residents that may erode DeleteMe's value proposition in its largest market.

## Privacy Bee
- Earned PCMag's first-ever perfect 5-star rating for a data removal service (August 6, 2026), along with Editors' Choice recognition — a significant marketing win.
- Claims industry-leading coverage of 1,128 data brokers as of August 5, 2026.
- Acquired EraseMe.app on August 12, 2026 — financial terms undisclosed; EraseMe.app visitors are now redirected to Privacy Bee, existing subscribers unaffected.
- Won All About Cookies 2026 Editors' Choice award in "Best for Digital Footprint Removal" category.
- Named a Cybersecurity Excellence Award recipient (July 2026).

## Incogni (by Surfshark)
- Running a Cybersecurity Awareness Week promotion: 58% off with a promo code, covered by 9to5Mac on October 24, 2026.
- Gizmodo's four-month hands-on test (as of October 2) logged 107 removal requests to 44 brokers, with 102 completed and 5 still in progress.
- AllAboutCookies reported 850 of 959 requests completed as of October 14, suggesting broader coverage scope than Gizmodo's test.
- Official coverage claim: 420+ data brokers, with recurring requests every 60–90 days.
- Deloitte limited-assurance verification covers Incogni's processes and request volume, but not whether brokers actually deleted data — a key caveat cited by reviewers.

## Optery (acquired by Surfshark, September 2026)
- Surfshark announced acquisition of a majority stake in Optery on approximately September 7, 2026; Optery will continue operating independently with its own team and product.
- Won Cyber Defense Magazine's 2026 Transformational Anti-Phishing Award (announced October 26, 2026).
- Partnered with GuidePoint Security to offer enterprise-grade personal data removal (announced September 29, 2026).
- Scored highly in independent Consumer Reports testing, outperforming DeleteMe and several other services.

## OneRep
- No major product or company announcements in the last 48 hours.
- Ongoing reputational issue: Krebs on Security reporting (2024) that OneRep's founder held ownership stakes in people-search sites continues to be cited in 2026 competitive comparisons.
- Coverage tiers: 317 people-search sites (Regular plan) and 876 data brokers (Pro plan) per current plan listings.

## Kanary
- Rebranded/pivoted product: Kanary Copilot is now the primary offering — a mobile app. New users can only access Copilot; the legacy web-based service is no longer available for new sign-ups.
- Pricing confusion persists across review sites: figures range from $9.99/month (Professional) up to $250/year (Core) and $500/year (Advanced) depending on source.
- Free tier (Copilot Community) offers limited functionality per TechRadar's assessment.
- Coverage: 300+ data brokers and people-search sites.

## Removaly
- Likely defunct: Multiple sources indicate removaly.com did not resolve as of July 5, 2026. Cloudwards previously noted removing Removaly from its recommendations due to the service shutting down.
- No recent activity, press releases, or product announcements found.

## Market Summary
The data broker opt-out industry saw two major M&A moves in recent months (Privacy Bee acquiring EraseMe.app; Surfshark acquiring majority stake in Optery), signaling a maturing market where distribution and coverage breadth are key differentiators. California's DROP platform deadline of October 29, 2026 — requiring brokers to complete their first full consumer deletion cycle — marks a pivotal regulatory moment that could reduce urgency for paid removal services among CA residents and pressure incumbents to differentiate beyond simple opt-outs.
