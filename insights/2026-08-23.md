# Competitor Insights — 2026-08-23

## Privacy Bee
- **Historic milestone**: PCMag awarded Privacy Bee a perfect 5.0/5.0 stars + Editors' Choice on August 5 — the first time any data removal service has earned a perfect score in PCMag's 44-year history of testing; reviewer Neil J. Rubenking titled the review "Unbeatable Data Protection With Industry-Leading Broker Coverage"
- **Acquisition**: On August 12, Privacy Bee acquired EraseMe.app, absorbing its removal technology and customer base; EraseMe.app users are being migrated to Privacy Bee's platform
- Coverage now stands at **1,128 data brokers** plus removals from 185,000+ custom sites — widest coverage in the market
- The PCMag win + acquisition in the same week represents a dual-channel aggressive growth push (earned media + inorganic expansion)

## DeleteMe
- Notably downgraded to **3.5/5.0 stars** in the August 5 PCMag roundup — the lowest rating among major services and well behind Optery (4.5) and Incogni (4.5)
- Continues charging $129/year with ~750 broker coverage and 90-day re-check cadence; the gap vs. competitors on price-per-broker is widening
- No free tier and the PCMag demotion leaves it exposed on both the value and trust dimensions

## Incogni
- Confirmed independent **Deloitte audit** verifying removal practices — a credibility differentiator no other service in the category currently holds
- PCMag rating of **4.5/5.0** holds steady; positions well against DeleteMe's 3.5 demotion
- Standard plan from $6.49/month (annual); Unlimited plan at $13.49/month with custom removals by privacy specialists; Family plan available

## Optery
- **Canada expansion** now live (excluding Quebec); becoming the broadest geographically available service alongside Privacy Bee
- New **Map Blurring** feature for Extended and Ultimate plan subscribers: users can request blurring of street-level imagery of their home on Google Maps and Apple Maps directly via Optery dashboard
- Maintains PCMag rating of **4.5/5.0**; free tier intact, making it the most accessible entry point in the market
- Covers 600+ sites with automated removals; free exposure report available to all users

## OneRep
- Trust headwinds persist: multiple reviewers in August 2026 continue flagging that OneRep's CEO co-founded Nuwber and other people-search sites — the exact sites OneRep claims to remove users from
- Covers only 235 people-search sites; one of the narrowest coverages in the market
- Ongoing negative press creates churn risk and opens the door for competitors to run targeted trust-focused campaigns

## Kanary
- PCMag rating of **4.0/5.0** as of August 5 roundup; holding mid-tier position
- No new product announcements detected in the past 48 hours

## Removaly
- Remains shut down; no news of a revival or asset sale

## Regulatory & Market Context
- **California DROP enforcement** — August 1, 2026 is active: 600+ registered data brokers must process DROP deletion requests at least every 45 days or face $200/day/consumer fines
- August 7, 2026: CalPrivacy Board convened to discuss **compliance audit regulations** and additional DROP amendments; 345,000+ deletion requests submitted to date
- The DROP compliance wave is driving significant consumer awareness and new demand for managed opt-out services
- An estimated **26 million people** are expected to start using a data broker removal service in 2026

## Market Summary
Privacy Bee is the week's dominant story: a historic PCMag perfect score followed immediately by the EraseMe.app acquisition signals a well-resourced push to consolidate the market at the premium end, while DeleteMe's PCMag demotion to 3.5 stars and Incogni's Deloitte audit credibility confirm the battle lines are increasingly drawn on trust and editorial validation. California's DROP enforcement, now in its active phase, is the macro tailwind lifting the entire category.
