## Regulatory / Market Context
- **CalPrivacy DROP audit proposal (Nov 13):** California's privacy agency opened a public comment period (open until Jan 6, 2027) on rules requiring registered data brokers to undergo independent third-party audits starting January 1, 2028, and every three years thereafter. This will raise compliance costs for brokers and may increase demand for removal services.
- **DROP adoption milestone:** As of November 1, 2026, over 500,000 consumers have submitted DROP requests covering nearly 3 million individual identifiers to 680 registered brokers — growing consumer uptake of California's centralized opt-out system.
- **Faster deletion deadlines:** Proposed rules would reduce deletion processing from 45 to 30 days; AB 883 codifies this as a statutory requirement.
- **CCPA opt-out signals:** CalPrivacy board voted 5-0 on Nov 6 to begin formal rulemaking for amendments to opt-out preference signal regulations — impacting how cookie banners and GPC signals are handled industry-wide.

## Market Summary
The data removal market is consolidating rapidly — Surfshark's September acquisition of Optery and Privacy Bee's absorption of EraseMe.app signal M&A momentum — while California's tightening DROP audit rules and shortened deletion deadlines create both regulatory tailwinds (driving consumer awareness) and operational pressure on data brokers. Incogni is leaning hard into Black Friday discounting and its Deloitte-verified credibility claim as the key seasonal push heading into Q4 2026.
