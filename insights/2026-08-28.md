# Competitor Insights — 2026-08-28

## Privacy Bee
- **PCMag Perfect 5-Star Rating (Aug 6)**: Privacy Bee became the first and only data removal service to earn a perfect 5.0/5.0 rating from PCMag, earning Editors' Choice recognition. PCMag called it "the ultimate first choice" and credited it with "the most extensive data broker coverage on the market today." No service in the category had previously exceeded 4.5 stars in PCMag's 44 years of testing.
- **Acquisition of EraseMe.app (Aug 12)**: Privacy Bee acquired EraseMe.app, a personal data removal tool, folding its customer base and technology into the Privacy Bee platform. The acquisition strengthens Privacy Bee's automated identification, removal monitoring, and re-exposure detection capabilities. EraseMe.app visitors are now redirected to Privacy Bee.
- Now covers **1,128 data brokers** and over **185,000 custom sites** — the broadest coverage in the space.

## DeleteMe
- Rated **3.5/5 stars** by PCMag (as of Aug 5), a drop in relative standing as Privacy Bee and Optery have pulled ahead in review rankings.
- Noted as a mid-level performer in effectiveness testing, though it retains strong brand recognition and is US-headquartered with solid people-search site coverage.
- Pricing remains at $129+/year per person.

## Incogni
- Holds a **4.5/5 PCMag rating**, tied with Optery as the second-best rated service.
- Covers 420+ data brokers with continuous automated monitoring and re-submission.
- Pricing: $6.49/month billed annually (~$77.88/year) — among the most affordable mid-tier options.

## Optery
- Rated **4.5/5 by PCMag**, tied for second. Has processed over 10 million opt-out requests to date.
- Offers "Expanded Reach" feature adding 250+ additional brokers, bringing total coverage to 640+ brokers.
- Pricing starts at $39/year for basic plans; higher tiers include human privacy agents.
- Historically performed best in Consumer Reports testing among people-search removal services.

## Kanary
- Rated **4.0/5 by PCMag**. Strongest performance in the people-search category, with removal success rate exceeding 90% within 14 days on those specific sites.
- Remains a solid mid-tier option; no major product announcements this week.

## OneRep
- No new product announcements. Ongoing trust concerns persist due to the CEO's ownership stake in Nuwber (a data broker) and his founding of over a dozen people-search sites — the same type of sites OneRep claims to remove users from.
- Covers 318 data broker sites on its basic plan and up to 877 on its pro plan.

## Removaly
- No significant news or announcements found in the last 48 hours.

## EasyOptOuts (Emerging Competitor)
- Gaining attention as the cheapest recurring data removal service at **$19.99/year** — significantly below all major players.
- Continues to attract cost-conscious users who want automated opt-outs without premium pricing.

## Regulatory Context: California DROP Goes Live
- **August 1, 2026**: California's DELETE Act Delete Request and Opt-Out Platform (DROP) became fully operational. All 600+ registered California data brokers must now check DROP every 45 days, download consumer deletion lists, and process removals.
- Penalty: **$200 per request per day** for non-compliance starting August 1.
- This is a major regulatory tailwind for all opt-out services — it validates the category and puts pressure on brokers to honor removals. Services like DeleteMe, Optery, and Incogni are directly benefiting from increased consumer awareness triggered by DROP's launch media coverage.

## Market Summary
This week's dominant story is Privacy Bee's double-punch of a first-ever PCMag perfect score and the acquisition of EraseMe.app, consolidating its position as the category leader at a critical moment when California's DROP platform going live is driving a surge of consumer interest in data removal services. The regulatory momentum from DROP is raising all boats in the space, but Privacy Bee's aggressive expansion — in coverage, ratings, and M&A — puts significant pressure on DeleteMe (now rated below Incogni and Optery) and creates a two-tier market between premium full-service providers and budget options like EasyOptOuts.
