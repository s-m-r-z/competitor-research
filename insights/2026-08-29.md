# Competitor Insights — 2026-08-29

## Privacy Bee
- Continues to dominate post-PCMag perfect 5.0/5.0 rating (Aug 6). A fresh head-to-head review on CyberInsider (Incogni vs Privacy Bee) validates the lead, placing Privacy Bee above Incogni in coverage breadth and overall score.
- No new announcements today; momentum from the EraseMe.app acquisition (Aug 12) and record coverage of 1,128 data brokers continues to drive inbound attention.
- PCMag ratings snapshot remains: Privacy Bee 5.0, Optery 4.5, Incogni 4.5, Kanary 4.0, DeleteMe 3.5.

## Incogni
- Phandroid published a fresh **Incogni vs Kanary 2026** comparison on August 28, positioning Incogni as the better value pick for most users due to its lower price point (~$6.49/month billed annually) and independently verified removal claims across 420+ brokers.
- Separately, the CyberInsider Incogni vs Privacy Bee head-to-head reaffirms Incogni's strengths in affordability and transparent reporting, though it ranks second to Privacy Bee on coverage.
- Incogni continues to rank #1 overall in some roundup reviews (e.g., Deepak Gupta's Top 5 Data Removal Services 2026) that weight price-to-performance over raw broker count.

## Kanary
- Featured prominently in the Phandroid Incogni vs Kanary comparison (Aug 28). Kanary is described as the better fit for users wanting a more hands-on, UI-driven experience with strong people-search site removal (300+ brokers, continuous monitoring).
- Rated 4.0/5 by PCMag. No pricing changes or new features announced.

## DeleteMe
- Rated 3.5/5 by PCMag; maintains brand recognition but trails Optery, Incogni, and Privacy Bee in review rankings.
- Continues to cover ~750 brokers with a manual, risk-prioritized approach and a 90-day re-check cycle.
- No new announcements found in the last 24–48 hours.

## Optery
- Rated 4.5/5 by PCMag, tied with Incogni. OneRep's own blog published a **DeleteMe vs Optery comparison** positioning Optery as the stronger performer in recent testing — notable as a competitor acknowledging Optery's quality.
- Covers 640+ brokers with its Expanded Reach add-on. Pricing starts at $39/year. No new feature announcements today.

## OneRep
- No new product announcements. Trust concerns remain a persistent negative signal: CEO's founding of 12+ people-search sites and ownership stake in Nuwber (a data broker) continue to surface in independent reviews and comparisons.
- OneRep's blog is actively publishing comparison content (DeleteMe vs Optery, How to Remove Yourself from Data Brokers 2026), signaling a content-marketing push to drive top-of-funnel traffic.

## Removaly
- **Confirmed shut down.** Increditools and other review sites are now actively publishing "Top Removaly Alternatives" content, indicating the closure is widely recognized. No relaunch activity detected. Former users are being directed toward Incogni, DeleteMe, and Optery.

## EasyOptOuts (Emerging Competitor)
- No new announcements. Continues to position on price ($19.99/year) as the budget alternative to full-service providers.

## Regulatory Context: California DROP Enforcement Deepens
- As of August 7, **345,000+ deletion requests** have been submitted through California's DROP platform since it opened to consumers on January 1, 2026.
- The CalPrivacy Board convened to discuss **proposed DROP compliance audit regulations**, signaling the agency is moving toward formal enforcement mechanisms beyond the existing $200/request/day penalty.
- Data brokers' mandatory 45-day processing cycle is now fully active (started August 1). The first compliance window closes mid-September, meaning the initial wave of enforced deletions is imminent.
- This regulatory pressure continues to be a strong tailwind for all opt-out services, as DROP's media coverage drives consumer awareness and subscription intent.

## Market Summary
Today's landscape is defined by consolidation at the top — Privacy Bee holds a clear lead in review ratings while Incogni competes aggressively on price-to-performance — and by Removaly's confirmed shutdown, which is redirecting a meaningful pool of privacy-conscious consumers toward the remaining players. California's DROP enforcement deepening with a compliance audit framework on the horizon adds urgency for consumers and further legitimizes the paid opt-out category heading into Q4.
