# Competitor Insights — 2026-11-25

## DeleteMe
- Ended enrollment for international plans around September 21, 2026; service is now limited to US residents
- Local media coverage (Gulf Coast News, Nov 20, 2026) featured a Florida resident who used DeleteMe to cut spam calls; company rep claims ~85% of found information removed within four months
- Broker count claims vary across sources (750–969 sites depending on source), suggesting marketing figures are inconsistent
- Faces growing competitive pressure from California's free state-run DROP platform

## Incogni (Surfshark Group)
- Running an early Black Friday 2026 promotion: 58% off Standard plan (~$6.79/mo billed at $95.88/yr per Cybernews); Black Friday officially November 27, Cyber Monday November 30
- Became the first data removal service to have claims independently verified by Deloitte (limited assurance)
- Covers 420+ data broker and people-search sites
- Now part of a two-service Surfshark Group lineup alongside Optery (see below)

## Privacy Bee
- PCMag awarded Privacy Bee its first-ever perfect 5-star rating and Editors' Choice on August 5, 2026 — placing it above Incogni (4.5 stars) and Optery (4.5 stars)
- Claims coverage of 1,128 data brokers and 185,000+ custom sites as of that date
- Acquired EraseMe.app to expand data removal technology and ongoing privacy monitoring (terms not disclosed)
- TechRadar calls it one of the strongest "set-and-forget" platforms in 2026; critics note higher-tier plans required for full coverage

## Kanary
- Transitioned new users from web service to a mobile app called Kanary Copilot; existing web users retain legacy access
- Mobile app coverage is narrow — reportedly around 10 websites at launch, far fewer than the web product's ~209 people-search sites
- Claims broader scope including social media, search engines, and AI/LLMs, but independent reviewers (TechWalla) say evidence is lacking
- Positive note: Kanary says it helped shut down GoButler, a people-search site, following thousands of member complaints
- Security.org gives DeleteMe a 9.1/10 vs Kanary in direct comparison; TechRadar labels Kanary a niche pick for streamers/gamers

## Optery (Surfshark Group)
- Surfshark Group acquired a majority stake in Optery, announced September 7, 2026; financial terms undisclosed
- Optery operates as a standalone service — separate team, product, and technology from Incogni
- SecurityHero 30-day test (published ~Nov 20, 2026): Optery found personal data on 119 sites, removed 73 within 7 days on Ultimate plan
- No immediate service changes for existing customers per Surfshark's announcement

## OneRep
- PCWorld review published November 12, 2026: after one week, 6 of 47 profiles removed, 41 still in progress
- Claims removal from 880 data brokers (vendor figure; unverified independently)
- Sends opt-out requests after consumer consent but cannot directly inspect broker records

## Regulatory / Market Context
- **California DROP platform**: Launched January 1, 2026; mandatory broker compliance began August 1, 2026. Free for California residents — a single deletion request transmitted to 580+ registered data brokers. Over 300,000 Californians had signed up by June 2026. Brokers must check and process requests every 45 days. Only state-run tool of its kind in the US; Vermont H.211 is using DROP as a model.
- **Other emerging services**: Aura and Reputation Defender (Norton, ~$9.95/mo) continue as supplementary market players; no new startup launches identified this week.

## Market Summary
The data broker opt-out market is consolidating rapidly — Surfshark Group now controls both Incogni and Optery, while Privacy Bee leads independent players with a perfect PCMag score and an acquisition of EraseMe.app. California's free DROP platform, now actively enforced since August 2026, is the biggest structural threat to paid removal services, with over 300,000 users already enrolled.
