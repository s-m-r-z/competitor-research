# Competitor Insights — 2026-08-24

## Privacy Bee
- **PCMag Perfect 5-Star Rating (Aug 6)**: Privacy Bee became the first and only data removal service in PCMag's 44-year history to earn a perfect 5/5 stars and Editors' Choice recognition. Previous category leaders (Optery, Incogni) top out at 4.5 stars. PCMag credits Privacy Bee with "the most extensive data broker coverage on the market today," covering 1,128 data brokers and 185,000+ custom sites.
- **Acquisition of EraseMe.app (Aug 12)**: Privacy Bee acquired EraseMe.app, a personal data removal service with proprietary technology for identifying exposure and managing removals. EraseMe.app customers gain access to Privacy Bee's monitoring and broader coverage; new EraseMe.app visitors are redirected to Privacy Bee. This consolidation further widens Privacy Bee's competitive moat in both technology and market share.
- Current PCMag rankings: Privacy Bee 5.0 > Optery 4.5 = Incogni 4.5 > Kanary 4.0 > DeleteMe 3.5.

## DeleteMe
- Rated 3.5/5 by PCMag — notably below peers Optery and Incogni (both 4.5) and now well behind Privacy Bee (5.0).
- Rated a "mid-level performer" in independent testing with ~65% removal rates; coverage is ~750+ data brokers at ~$10.75/month ($129/year).
- No significant new product announcements or pricing changes found in the last 48 hours.

## Incogni
- Holds a 4.5/5 PCMag rating; pricing unchanged: $7.99/month (annual) or $15.98/month rolling for Standard (420+ brokers); Unlimited plan at $14.99/month (annual) for unlimited custom removals.
- Notable differentiator: the only major data removal service independently audited by Deloitte, verifying its removal process operates as described.
- Independent tests show ~42% removal rate within one week, slower than Optery (~68%) but with fast initial turnaround.
- No new announcements found in the last 48 hours.

## Optery
- PCMag rating: 4.5/5. Pricing: Core $39/yr (370+ brokers), Extended $149/yr (545+ brokers), Ultimate $249/yr (640+ brokers + unlimited custom requests with human escalation).
- Top performer in independent testing at ~68% removal rate; unique "live screenshots" feature provides proof of actual removals.
- Free tier available for pre-purchase scanning — a strong conversion tool.
- No new announcements found in the last 48 hours.

## Kanary
- PCMag rating: 4.0/5. Uses a hybrid automated/manual approach achieving 95–100% removal rates by day 90.
- Offers monthly rescans and enterprise-grade options, differentiating from consumer-only peers.
- No new announcements found in the last 48 hours.

## OneRep
- Fully automated; Basic plan covers 318 sites, Pro covers 877 sites.
- Ongoing trust/credibility issue: CEO founded 12+ people-search sites and retains an ownership stake in Nuwber (a data broker), creating an inherent conflict of interest that multiple review outlets continue to flag.
- No new product announcements found in the last 48 hours.

## Removaly
- Confirmed shut down; no longer operating. Multiple sources directing former customers to alternatives.

## EasyOptOuts (Emerging Competitor)
- Budget leader at $19.99/year; achieves ~65% removal rate across ~100 brokers (3 automated scans/year) — tied with Optery as a top performer in Consumer Reports testing.
- Primary weakness: no detailed reporting. Addressing this gap could significantly increase competitive pressure on premium-priced services.

## Regulatory / Market Context — California DROP Enforcement (Aug 1)
- **Critical market development**: California's Delete Act DROP platform entered full enforcement on August 1, 2026. Data brokers must now process deletion requests every 45 days or face $200/day/request fines.
- DROP allows California residents to submit one free deletion request reaching all 500+ registered data brokers — a potentially significant value substitute for paid removal services among California users.
- California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA) has launched a "Data Broker Strike Force" to pursue enforcement.
- Impact on industry: paid services may need to differentiate more aggressively on non-California brokers, speed, reporting quality, and ongoing monitoring to retain California-based subscribers.

## Market Summary
Privacy Bee is the week's dominant story, cementing a market leadership position via a perfect PCMag rating and the strategic acquisition of EraseMe.app — a rare double-event that may pressure mid-tier competitors (particularly DeleteMe at 3.5 stars) to accelerate product improvements. The August 1 California DROP enforcement deadline adds structural pressure across the industry, as free government-backed opt-outs now compete directly with paid services for the state's large consumer base.
