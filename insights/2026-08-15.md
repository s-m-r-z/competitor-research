# Competitor Insights — 2026-08-15

## Privacy Bee
- **PCMag Perfect 5-Star Rating (Aug 6):** Privacy Bee became the first and only data removal service in PCMag's 44-year history to earn a perfect 5/5 Editors' Choice rating — leapfrogging Optery and Incogni (each at 4.5 stars) and pushing DeleteMe (3.5 stars) further down the rankings.
- **Acquires EraseMe.app (Aug 12):** Privacy Bee announced the acquisition of EraseMe.app, a personal data removal service with proprietary tooling for exposure identification and removal monitoring. EraseMe.app customers are being migrated to Privacy Bee; new visitors to EraseMe.app are redirected to Privacy Bee.
- **Coverage leader:** Now covers 1,128 data brokers and 185,000+ custom sites — the broadest coverage cited by any competitor in current reviews.

## Optery
- Named CNET's "Best Overall Personal Data Removal Service" for 2026, and has earned PCMag Editors' Choice every year from 2022–2026 (now rated 4.5 stars, behind Privacy Bee's new 5-star rating).
- Current pricing tiers: Core ($3.99/mo), Extended ($14.99/mo), Ultimate ($24.99/mo / ~$249/yr) — covering 600+ broker sites on the top plan with before-and-after verification screenshots.
- Differentiator remains verifiable, screenshot-based proof of removals, which no other top competitor offers as a core feature.

## DeleteMe
- No major product announcements or pricing changes detected in the last 48 hours.
- Maintains position as a well-established mid-tier option: 750+ data broker sites covered, quarterly human-reviewed reports, ~$129/year. PCMag rating dropped to 3.5 stars in the latest ranking cycle.
- California's DROP platform (see Market Summary) may reduce value proposition for California-based customers specifically.

## Incogni
- No specific product announcements in the last 48 hours, but remains strongly positioned in head-to-head comparisons vs. OneRep (Fossbytes, NordVPN, Cloudwards all published 2026 comparison articles favoring Incogni for individuals).
- Cited milestone: 200M+ successful data removals over three years, significantly outpacing OneRep (19M).
- Current PCMag rating: 4.5 stars. Broad coverage of both people-search sites and private/marketing data brokers is its key differentiator over OneRep.

## OneRep
- No new product announcements detected in the last 48 hours.
- Continues to be recommended primarily for families (supports more users per plan) but is consistently rated below Incogni for individual users due to narrower coverage (people-search sites only).
- OneRep's blog published a Privacy Bee review, a sign of increased competitive awareness of Privacy Bee's rising prominence.

## Kanary
- PCMag rating holds at 4.0 stars as of the August 5 ranking update.
- No major product news or announcements detected in the last 48 hours.

## Removaly
- Confirmed shutdown: Removaly has ceased active operations. Multiple third-party review sites (EarthWeb, Increditools) are now publishing "Removaly Alternatives" guides, with Incogni as the top recommended replacement.
- No active service for new customers; represents an opportunity to capture displaced Removaly users.

## Regulatory / Market: California DROP Platform
- **Major development (Aug 1, 2026):** California's DELETE Act "DROP" (Delete Request and Opt-Out Platform) went live operationally. As of August 1, all 600+ registered data brokers must check the platform and process deletion requests at least once every 45 days or face $200/day/request fines.
- Over 300,000 Californians have already enrolled in DROP since its January 2026 launch.
- **Industry implication:** DROP's single-click deletion for California residents partially automates what paid services offer for that geography. Services must now articulate value beyond California (nationwide broker coverage, ongoing re-removal, non-people-search brokers) to justify cost for California customers.

## Market Summary
Privacy Bee is dominating the competitive news cycle this week with a landmark PCMag perfect score followed immediately by the EraseMe.app acquisition, signaling aggressive consolidation and brand positioning — the rest of the market, particularly DeleteMe and Kanary, now faces a widening perception gap. Simultaneously, California's DROP platform going live marks a structural regulatory shift that could erode the value proposition of paid services for California residents and may accelerate competitive differentiation around non-California and non-people-search broker coverage.
