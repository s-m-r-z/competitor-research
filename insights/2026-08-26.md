## Regulatory & Market Context
- **California DROP enforcement live as of August 1, 2026:** 600+ registered data brokers must access DROP every 45 days and process deletion requests or face $200/day fines per consumer. 450,000+ Californians have submitted requests; one missed 45-day cycle produces ~$1.5B theoretical liability.
- **Stanford study (August 2026):** Only 9% of data brokers comply with California's privacy law — a headline that privacy services are likely to amplify in marketing.
- **CPPA rulemaking advancing (Aug 6–7 board meeting):** Formal rulemaking on Global Privacy Control (GPC) opt-out signals and new data broker audit requirements. New Jersey, Connecticut, and Vermont also advancing their own data broker frameworks.
- **Industry tailwind:** Growing compliance pressure is raising consumer awareness and increasing demand for automated opt-out services across the board.

## Market Summary
The California DROP enforcement deadline (August 1) and a damning Stanford study revealing only 9% data broker compliance are dominating the regulatory conversation this week, creating a strong tailwind for all opt-out services to market around. The notable structural story is Removaly's confirmed shutdown, which leaves its former customers actively shopping for alternatives — an acquisition opportunity for competitors running "Removaly alternatives" content.
