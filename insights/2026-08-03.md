# Competitor Insights — 2026-08-03

## California DROP — Day 3 of Enforcement

- Confirmed figures: **300,000+ California consumers** have registered deletion requests since the platform opened January 1, 2026 — up from 215,000 reported yesterday, suggesting a surge of signups around the August 1 enforcement start
- 600+ registered data brokers must now process queued requests within 90 days or face **$200 per request per day** — theoretical maximum liability exceeds $1.5 billion on a single missed 45-day cycle
- CalPrivacy enforcement history adds context to how seriously the agency treats non-compliance: S&P Global fined $62,600, Datamasters $45,000 (plus ordered to stop selling CA residents' data), Accurate Append $55,400, and National Public Data (Jerico Pictures) $46,000 — all for registration failures before August 1
- The Data Broker Enforcement Strike Force (launched November 2025) has already issued multiple decisions; enforcement escalation post-August 1 is explicit agency policy, not gradual ramp-up
- No major competitor issued DROP-related marketing for a third consecutive day; first-mover content opportunity remains unclaimed

## Market Summary

Day 3 of California DROP enforcement finds the entire category still silent on the biggest regulatory story in the industry's history, while the consumer signups surge (215K → 300K+ in two days) validates that demand is real and accelerating. Optery continues to consolidate its "clear winner" narrative in third-party review content, while Incogni's 245M removal requests milestone and Deloitte audit give it the strongest trust floor — both represent immediate messaging opportunities for competitors.
