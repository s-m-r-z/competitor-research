# Competitor Insights — 2026-10-28

## DeleteMe
- **International enrollment closed (September 2026):** DeleteMe has shut down enrollment on its international plans, limiting new sign-ups to US customers only.
- **California DROP impact:** California's Delete Request and Opt-out Platform (DROP) went live January 1, 2026, with brokers required to process requests since August 1, 2026 — this may shift DeleteMe's automation strategy as manual requests become less necessary for CA residents.
- **Pricing unchanged:** $129/year for one person, $229 for two, $329 for a household of four.
- **Mixed performance data:** Consumer Reports testing scored DeleteMe at 27% removal effectiveness, placing it near the bottom of tested services. Some mainstream publications (e.g., Wirecutter) still rank it highly.
- **Coverage claims inconsistent:** DeleteMe advertises removal from 750+ data brokers, but independent analysis suggests automated coverage is closer to ~100 high-priority sites with the rest handled via custom requests.

## Privacy Bee
- **PCMag perfect 5-star rating (August 2026):** Privacy Bee became the first and only data removal service to earn a perfect 5.0 "Exemplary" rating and Editors' Choice from PCMag — a significant marketing milestone.
- **Acquisition of EraseMe App (August 12, 2026):** Privacy Bee acquired EraseMe App, expanding its personal data monitoring capabilities and data broker coverage.
- **Broad coverage:** Now covers 1,128 data brokers and 185,000+ supported custom sites, positioning it as the widest-coverage option in the market.
- **No independent removal effectiveness data** found in current reviews — PCMag rating is process/feature-based rather than outcome-based.

## Kanary

- **Independent testing:** Consumer Reports testing found 34% removal effectiveness — above DeleteMe (27%) but well below top performers. A 2025 PoPETs academic study ranked Kanary last at 23.4%.

## Optery
- **Major ownership change (September 2026):** Surfshark (the VPN company) announced acquisition of a majority stake in Optery. The company will reportedly retain its independent team and technology.
- **Strong independent test results:** Optery performed well in Consumer Reports testing and a recent October 13, 2026 comparison covering 19 major people-search sites.
- **Strategic implication:** Surfshark/Nord Security's move into data removal (already owning Incogni) creates a dominant portfolio. Optery acquisition gives them a second brand targeting different market segments.

## Incogni

- **Deloitte audit results:** A Deloitte limited assurance report confirms Incogni covers 420+ data brokers and did not sell customer data during 2024. Requests are re-sent every 60 days (public brokers) and 90 days (private brokers).
- **Custom Removals feature:** Introduced in 2025 for sites outside its automated broker list — still actively promoted in reviews.

## Industry / Regulatory
- **California DROP fully operational:** The state's unified deletion request platform now reaches 600+ registered brokers. AB 883 (pending) would shorten the DROP response window from 45 to 30 days.
- **SB 923 signed:** Expands CCPA deletion rights to cover third-party-sourced data — raising the compliance bar for data brokers and potentially benefiting removal services.
- **AB 1542 vetoed:** Governor Newsom vetoed a bill that would have outright banned sale/sharing of sensitive personal information.
- **Radaris domain seized:** Radaris's domain has been transferred by court order — a notable enforcement action against a major people-search site. Services that covered Radaris removals will need to update their supported sites lists.
- **Surfshark now controls two brands:** With the majority stake in Optery alongside Incogni, Surfshark/Nord Security is emerging as the dominant operator in the automated data removal space.

## Market Summary
The most significant development this week is Surfshark's acquisition of a majority stake in Optery, which — combined with its existing Incogni service — makes it the largest operator in the data broker opt-out market by brand count, while Privacy Bee continues to gain credibility through its PCMag 5-star rating and EraseMe acquisition. Regulatory tailwinds from California's DROP platform and SB 923 are raising the baseline expectation for broker compliance, potentially commoditizing the simpler end of automated removal services.
