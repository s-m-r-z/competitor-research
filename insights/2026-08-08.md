# Competitor Insights — 2026-08-08

## Regulatory Environment (Critical — Affects All Players)
- **California DROP is now in day 8 of mandatory deletion processing** — 614 registered data brokers are required to access the platform at least every 45 days and process verified requests within 45 days; $200/day fines apply with no cure period; the CPPA is actively monitoring compliance through its Data Broker Strike Force
- **Hundreds of thousands of Californians** have now submitted DROP deletion requests since the platform's January 2026 launch; a single DROP request reaches all 614 registered brokers simultaneously, creating significant automated removal volume industry-wide
- **New Jersey's data broker law (enacted June 30, 2026)** extends regulatory exposure beyond California, targeting both brokers and upstream data suppliers — multi-state compliance pressure is building
- The DROP precedent is compressing the value proposition of services that only submit removal requests to California-registered brokers; competitive differentiation is shifting toward non-public/B2B broker coverage and non-California jurisdictions

## Privacy Bee
- Continues to ride momentum from its **PCMag perfect 5.0-star Editors' Choice rating (announced August 6)** — the first perfect score in the category in PCMag's 44-year testing history; expect sustained PR and paid media amplification through this week
- Current PCMag leaderboard as of August 6–7: Privacy Bee 5.0 ✦ Optery 4.5 ✦ Incogni 4.5 ✦ Kanary 4.0 ✦ DeleteMe 3.5
- Features weekly privacy audits and dashboard risk rankings; contacts **1,000+ data brokers** including Google search result removal, house blur, tracker-blocking browser extension, private search, and Gmail inbox scan
- Positioned as the "always-on" premium option; still one of the pricier services but its depth of features is sustaining top-tier independent ratings

## Optery
- **PCMag Editors' Choice at 4.5 stars** (5 consecutive years, 2022–2026), though displaced from the #1 slot by Privacy Bee's 5-star jump — first time since 2022 it does not hold the top PCMag rank
- **CNET named Optery "Best Overall Data Removal Service of 2026"** — strong dual-award position despite PCMag demotion
- Open-source data broker directory (launched August 7) on GitHub (`optery/optery-data-brokers-directory`) covering 640+ brokers with opt-out links and step-by-step guides — strategic transparency play generating developer and press credibility
- Pricing tiers: Core ($3.99/month billed annually, 120+ sites) through Extended ($10.99/month billed annually, 370+ sites); SOC 2 Type II certified
- No new announcements in the past 24 hours; focus appears to be on sustained organic content around the directory launch

## DeleteMe
- No new announcements or pricing changes in the last 24–48 hours
- Most recent independent reviews (last updated **August 4, 2026**) continue to rank DeleteMe highly for coverage breadth: **750–850+ brokers** covered with human-led concierge approach
- Pricing remains $129/year ($10.75/month) for one person; family plans available
- PCMag rating of 3.5 stars remains the weakest among named competitors — a growing messaging liability as head-to-head comparison content proliferates
- DROP compliance creates new pressure: automated, policy-backed deletion requests from California users may replicate some of DeleteMe's core manual-removal value for California residents at zero marginal cost

## Incogni
- No major announcements in the past 24–48 hours; comparison articles updated **August 5, 2026**
- **Custom Removals feature** (launched 2025) allows subscribers to submit URLs to websites outside the automated broker list for human-assisted takedowns — closing a key gap vs. broader-coverage competitors
- Pricing: **~$6.49–$7.99/month billed annually** (most affordable fully-automated option); **55% off promotional codes** still circulating on TechRadar and affiliate channels — active seasonal pricing campaign
- Covers **184 brokers** on standard plan; **2,420+ brokers** on Unlimited plan; Deloitte-audited; family plans available (up to 5 members)
- Based in Netherlands (Surfshark brand); GDPR-aligned legal demand framework used for opt-outs

## Kanary
- **Product pivot confirmed: Kanary Copilot is now the primary offering** — iOS-only mobile app (Android waitlist open); the web platform remains available to existing users only; new customers are directed exclusively to the app
- Copilot uses a guided, bite-sized task format rather than "set-and-forget" automation — differentiating on user engagement but sacrificing convenience vs. Incogni/Optery
- Backed by **Mozilla Foundation** (key trust signal); covers **1,000+ sites** scanned (broad breadth), with 300+ broker automated removal coverage; 90%+ removal success rate within 14 days on tracked sites
- Pricing: $16.99/month or ~$179.88/year; **14-day free trial** — longest trial period of any named competitor
- PCMag rating: 4.0 stars; the iOS-only limitation may dampen growth until Android launches

## Emerging / Adjacent Players

## Market Summary
California DROP's enforcement entering week 2 is the dominant structural force shaping the competitive landscape: services that differentiate primarily on state-registered broker coverage will face margin compression as DROP commoditizes that value for Californians. Privacy Bee's freshly minted perfect PCMag 5-star rating (August 6) continues to generate category attention, while Kanary's pivot to a mobile-first app and OneRep's expansion into non-public B2B brokers signal that the next competitive battleground is coverage of the brokers DROP does not reach.
