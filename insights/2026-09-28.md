# Competitor Insights — 2026-09-28

## Privacy Bee
- **PCMag Perfect 5-Star Rating** (announced August 6, 2026) continues to drive significant press and referral traffic — no prior data removal service in PCMag's 44-year history achieved a perfect score. Privacy Bee now sits above Optery and Incogni (both at 4.5 stars) as PCMag's top recommendation in the category.
- Coverage reaches **1,128 data brokers** and supports removals from more than **185,000 custom sites** — cited as the broadest in the market.
- Competitive positioning sharpened around high-risk users (executives, stalking victims, public figures) with aggressive escalation tactics (legal threats, regulatory complaints) when brokers ignore standard opt-out requests.
- No new product or pricing announcements found in the last 24–48 hours.

## Optery

## Regulatory / Market
- **California DROP (Delete Request and Opt-out Platform)** August 1, 2026 mandate now fully active: all registered data brokers must check DROP **at least every 45 days** to retrieve and process deletion requests. This is the first government-run opt-out clearinghouse of its kind and continues to put commoditization pressure on paid removal services.
- California DROP enables a single request to reach **600+ registered data brokers** for free, reducing the perceived value proposition of subscription removal services that charge for breadth alone.
- **Data broker market** projected to exceed $440 billion by 2032 — long-term tailwind for the entire sector.

## Market Summary
The dominant story this week is Surfshark's consolidation play — owning both Incogni and Optery now gives it two top-5 removal services and positions it as the most vertically integrated privacy suite vendor. The simultaneous rise of Privacy Bee to PCMag's top rating and California's DROP mandate going fully operational signal a bifurcating market: premium differentiation at the top (breadth, legal escalation, AI-era coverage) and free/government alternatives commoditizing the bottom tier.
