# Competitor Insights — 2026-09-02

## Incogni
- **New "Protect" bundle plan launched** at $20.74/month — packages the existing Unlimited plan with Coveron, NordVPN's identity-protection service; this is a meaningful product expansion that bundles data removal with identity monitoring, raising ARPU and making Incogni competitive with premium security suites like Aura and McAfee+
- Now contacts 420+ data brokers (up from previously reported numbers); recently added **custom removal requests** feature allowing users to request deletions from specific sites outside the default broker list
- Aggressive upsell architecture: three tiers (Standard → Unlimited → Protect) give Incogni a wide revenue-per-user band and reduced churn for price-conscious shoppers

## DeleteMe
- No new announcements or pricing changes found in the last 24–48 hours
- Continues manual, risk-prioritized approach covering 122 sites with quarterly human-reviewed PDF reports; PCMag 3.5-star rating remains a competitive headwind; no indication of a product push to match Incogni's bundle strategy

## Regulatory Environment — California DROP Enforcement Week 1
- **Only ~30% of registered California data brokers processed DROP requests in the first week of enforcement** (August 1 enforcement start), despite 450,000+ consumer deletion requests already submitted through the platform — reported at California Privacy Protection Agency's (CPPA) August 2026 board meeting
- **SB 361 amendment** (effective 2026): unverifiable deletion requests must now be treated as valid opt-outs with a 45-day compliance window rather than being dismissed; also adds new required disclosures for sensitive data categories (sexual orientation, union membership, citizenship status)
- **CalPrivacy fee increases**: the board approved higher data broker registration and platform access fees citing rising administrative costs — increased friction and cost for brokers may accelerate voluntary market exits, reducing the size of the broker universe that services like DeleteMe and Incogni need to cover
- **Strategic implication**: the 70% non-compliance rate in DROP's first enforcement week is a marketing opportunity for all opt-out services — consumer-facing messaging around "brokers aren't complying, you need a service" is now substantiated by official CPPA data

## Market Summary
Incogni's new Protect bundle (data removal + identity protection at ~$21/month) is today's headline competitive move, directly targeting the premium security-suite market dominated by Aura and LifeLock; it widens the gap between Incogni and rivals like DeleteMe and Optery that lack bundled identity monitoring. On the regulatory front, California DROP's dismal 30% first-week compliance rate — against 450,000+ submitted consumer requests — is a watershed moment that validates the value proposition of automated removal services and gives every competitor fresh ammunition for urgency-based marketing.
