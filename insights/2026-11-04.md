# Competitor Insights — 2026-11-04

## DeleteMe
- No major product announcements in the last 24–48 hours.
- Published November 2026 "Incognito" newsletter focusing on holiday-season scams (Black Friday, Cyber Monday, Giving Tuesday fraud); no service or pricing changes announced.
- Marketing continues to emphasize coverage of 750+ data brokers; independent studies (Consumer Reports 2024) have questioned effectiveness versus manual opt-outs.
- Pricing remains in the ~$95–$180/year range per third-party comparisons.

## Incogni
- Active Black Friday promotional discounts spotted on third-party coupon sites (up to 55% off), suggesting a seasonal push heading into November/December.
- Annual plan priced at ~$95.88/year; monthly at ~$15.98/month.
- Remains part of the NordVPN/Nord Security family (originally built by Surfshark team).
- A 2025 Privacy Enhancing Technologies Symposium peer-reviewed study ranked Incogni highest among four removal services tested, though it called the category "relatively ineffective" overall — a notable independent data point competitors can use.
- Coverage cited at 420+ broker sites.

## Privacy Bee
- No fresh product news found in the last 48 hours.
- Currently offers three personal plans: Essentials, Pro, and Signature; starting price ~$96/year per PCRisk.
- G2 rating of 4.47/5 from 25 reviews (as of July 2026); positive marks for breadth of automation and breach monitoring.
- Top user complaints: high subscription cost, limited dashboard transparency, slower pace of removals after initial phase.
- Some sources list pricing as "contact vendor," suggesting possible enterprise/custom pricing tier activity.

## Kanary
- Published a "2026 Product Brief" blog post (kanary.com/blog/2026-product-brief) — specific contents not indexed yet, but signals active product roadmap communication.
- Confirmed shift toward a mobile-first "Kanary Copilot" app experience; competitor OneRep claims Copilot covers only ~10 sites (treat with caution as competitor-sourced).
- Pricing inconsistency across sources: CrabClear (Sep 2026) lists Kanary Core at $250/year and Advanced at $500/year; earlier sources cited $9.99/month Professional tier — official pricing page should be verified directly.
- Known for stronger coverage of people-search sites versus broader private data broker categories.
- Claimed a notable win: data broker site GoButler went offline following Kanary escalations and member complaints.

## OneRep
- No new announcements found.
- Standard plan covers 319 broker sites; Pro plan covers 878 sites (people-search focus).
- Pricing: ~$8.33/month on annual plan (~$100/year), or $14.95/month billed monthly — competitive on price versus DeleteMe and Incogni.
- Ongoing concern: documented conflict-of-interest allegations linking OneRep's ownership to the data broker industry itself (originally surfaced by security journalist Brian Krebs); no resolution noted.

## Optery
- No new announcements found.
- Strongest independently-verified removal rate: Consumer Reports field test put Optery at 68% removal — highest of paid services tested.
- Free tier remains a differentiator: shows exposure data without requiring payment or card, unlike most competitors.
- Plan tiers: Core (~90 sites), Extended (~240), Ultimate (~370+), with an expanded option at 610+ sites.
- Criticism: does not independently rescan broker sites to verify removals; relies on broker-confirmed acknowledgments.
- Awards from CNET and PCMag cited in its own marketing.

## Removaly
- No new activity detected.
- Operational status flagged as "uncertain" by competitor PrivacyOn (April 2026) — independent confirmation unavailable.
- Coverage limited to ~51–60 broker sites; significantly smaller footprint than top-tier competitors.
- Founded 2020; positions itself as the only bootstrapped, US-based data removal service.
- Minimal third-party review coverage suggests low market visibility.

## Emerging Players
- **CyberForget** appears in multiple 2026 comparison articles alongside DeleteMe, Incogni, and Optery — indicates growing market presence; positions itself on full opt-out lifecycle automation (scan → submit → verify → monitor → resubmit).
- **OffList** and **PrivacyOn** also surfaced in comparison content, suggesting continued new entrant activity in the space.

## Regulatory / Market Context
- **California DROP** platform surpassed 300,000 sign-ups; active broker processing obligations began August 1, 2026. 580+ brokers must process deletion requests every 45 days or face $200/day penalties per request.
- **Fee increase**: CalPrivacy raised annual data broker registration fees from $6,000 to $9,500 for 2027 — cost pressure on smaller brokers may reshape the addressable market for removal services.
- **Free government alternative**: DROP only covers California residents, keeping the paid removal service market intact in other states.

## Market Summary
The data broker opt-out space remains stable at the top (DeleteMe, Incogni, Optery dominating coverage), with Incogni making an early Black Friday pricing push and Kanary signaling continued product investment via a 2026 product brief. Regulatory tailwinds in California (DROP, fee increases) are adding pressure on data brokers but not yet displacing paid removal services, which retain value for non-California residents and for users wanting ongoing automated monitoring beyond what DROP provides.
